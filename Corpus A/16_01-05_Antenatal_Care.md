## 0.1 Pre-pregnancy counselling

**Immunisation**: rubella.

**Smoking cessation**
- ↓ovulation, abnormal sperm production
- 2x miscarriage risk
- Associated with preterm labour, fetal growth restriction, placenta praevia, abruption

**Weight**: aim BMI 18.5–<30; avoid contact sports.

**Supplementation — Folic acid**
- Essential for DNA/RNA synthesis
- Causes of deficiency: phenytoin, methotrexate, pregnancy, alcohol excess
- Consequences of deficiency: megaloblastic anaemia, neural tube defects

> [!info] Folic acid dosing
> - **0.4 mg/day** from >1 month preconception until ≥13 weeks (standard)
> - **5 mg/day** if: past neural tube defect, on antiepileptics, diabetic, obese, HIV+ve on co-trimoxazole prophylaxis, or sickle cell disease

**Supplementation — Vitamin D**: 10 mcg/day for all women, especially those with darker or covered skin.

**Alcohol**
- High levels → fetal alcohol syndrome, ↑miscarriage risk
- Minimal drinking (1–2 units/week) not shown to adversely affect fetus, but crosses placenta and may affect fetal brain

> [!info] Verified against the current NHMRC Australian Guidelines to Reduce Health Risks from Drinking Alcohol — **the Australian position is genuinely stricter than the UK's, not just differently worded**: NHMRC advises that women who are pregnant or planning a pregnancy should **not drink alcohol at all**, stating explicitly that no safe level of alcohol consumption during pregnancy has been identified — there is no Australian equivalent of the UK's "<1 unit/24h is low risk" framing, and the file's original UK threshold should not be used to reassure a patient that a small amount is fine. Even one or two drinks a week is described as potentially harmful to the developing fetus. This complete-abstinence message is a genuinely important, unambiguous point to convey clearly, not hedge.

**Recreational drugs**: associated with miscarriage, preterm birth, poor fetal development, intrauterine death.

**Pre-existing medical disorders**
- Poorly controlled → same or worse in pregnancy
- Well controlled → same or better
- Refer for specialist help ASAP

> [!danger] Medication changes prior to conception
> Change to reduce teratogenesis risk: **antiepileptics, ACE inhibitors, immune-modulators**. Avoid OTC medications as much as possible.

**Miscarriage**: spontaneous miscarriage occurs in 15–20% of all pregnancies (↑ with extremes of maternal age). Recurrent miscarriage = ≥3 consecutive miscarriages.

## 0.2 Physiological changes in pregnancy

**Hormonal**
- Progesterone: ↓muscle excitability, ↑body temperature
- Oestrogens (90% oestriol): ↑breast/nipple growth, water retention, protein synthesis
- Thyroid: ↑ in size
- Prolactin: ↑ throughout pregnancy
- Cortisol: ↑ output, but unbound levels remain constant

**Genital**
- Uterus: 100 g → 1.1 kg (hypertrophy ≤20w, then stretching)
- Vaginal discharge ↑ due to cervical ectopy, cell desquamation, ↑mucus production from vasocongested vagina

**Haemodynamic**

| System | Change |
|---|---|
| Plasma volume | Rises from 8–32w (50% above non-pregnant) |
| RBC | ↑30% if supplements taken; Hb falls due to dilution |
| WCC, platelets, ESR, cholesterol, β-globulin, fibrinogen | ↑ |
| Albumin, GGT | ↓ |
| Urea, creatinine | ↓ |
| Cardiac output | ↑ (SV ↑10%, HR ↑~15/min) |
| Peripheral resistance | ↓ |
| BP (esp. DBP) | ↓ in 2nd trimester, rises to non-pregnant levels by term |

- Varicose veins may form
- IVC compression from 20w → ↓CO by 30–40% (supine hypotension) → relieve with left lateral position or ~15° left tilt

**Other systems**
- Ventilation ↑40% (progesterone-mediated), O₂ uptake ↑20% only → SOB common
- Gut motility ↓ → constipation, delayed gastric emptying, lax oesophageal sphincter, heartburn
- Renal size ↑1cm length, GFR ↑60%, ↑pressure on bladder → ↑micturition
- Skin: linea nigra, nipple pigmentation, chloasma, palmar erythema, spider naevi, striae
- Hair shedding in puerperium
- Pregnancy tests may be +ve from 9 days post-conception; false +ve rate low

## 0.3 Antenatal care

### 0.3.1 1st antenatal (booking) visit

**History**
- FHx: DM, HTN, fetal abnormality, inheritable disease, twins
- Concurrent illness, FGM, VTE risk (± LMWH)
- GDM risk assessment → 75 g OGTT at 28w if BMI >30, previous macrosomia, 1st-degree relative with DM, or ethnic risk factors
  - If previous GDM: screen at 16w **and** again at 28w
- Past mental illness: if serious (schizophrenia, bipolar, self-harm) or past postnatal depression → antenatal assessment by perinatal mental health team
- Women born outside UK: ↑Hb problems, blood-borne viruses, pre-existing cardiac disease
- Ask about domestic violence and substance abuse in unsupported women

**Examination**: heart, lungs, BP, weight & BMI, abdomen, varicose veins, ± pap smear, FGM check.

**Tests**: Hb, blood group, infectious disease screen, sickle cell/thalassaemia screen; offer chromosomal/structural abnormality screening; 12w scan confirms EDD.

**Advice**: seatbelt use (above/below bump), antenatal classes, maternity benefits information.

> [!info] Verified — flight timing restrictions (commonly up to 36 weeks for a singleton pregnancy, earlier for multiples) and "fit to fly" letter requirements are generally set by individual airlines rather than being a UK-specific national rule, so this guidance is broadly applicable in Australia too — check the specific airline's policy for the current threshold given these vary between carriers and are reviewed periodically. Malaria-endemic area avoidance and the general travel precautions remain valid regardless of jurisdiction.

### 0.3.2 Later visits
- Discuss screening results, treat anaemia and UTI
- At each visit: check protein, BP, fundal height

> [!info] NICE visit schedule — **UK schedule, retained for reference only.** Australian antenatal visit scheduling follows RANZCOG and state maternity guidance and differs; **not verified against an Australian source** (see `PENDING_GUIDELINE_CHECKS.md` B32).
> Visits at <12, 16, 25, 28, 31, 34, 36, 38, 40, 41 weeks (25/31/40/41 only if primip). Verify against Australian shared antenatal care schedules.

- 28w: Hb, Rh antibodies — give anti-D if needed
- Most screening occurs in low-risk uncomplicated pregnancies
- Higher risk women: previously affected child, pre-existing DM, epilepsy

> [!info] Verified — that all antenatal screening is offered rather than mandatory, and can be declined by the pregnant person, is a universal principle of informed consent rather than a UK-specific policy, and applies identically in Australia — this doesn't need correcting for jurisdiction.

## 0.4 Structural abnormality screening

**Ultrasound**: detects pregnancy from ~5w gestation; used to date pregnancy.

**Nuchal translucency (NT) — 11–13w**
- Determines viability, dates pregnancy, diagnoses multiple pregnancy and chorionicity
- Screens for chromosomal abnormality via nuchal fold measurement + bloods
- ↑fetal NT may reflect fetal heart failure or serious cardiac/great vessel abnormality
- If bloods + NT give risk <1:150, invasive testing is offered

**Anomaly scan**
- Detailed US at 18–22w to detect structural malformations
- ~30 min; sensitivity varies

> [!info] Lethal anomalies detectable on anomaly scan
> Anencephaly, bilateral renal agenesis, some major cardiac abnormalities, trisomies 13 and 18

**Fetal echocardiography**: offered to those at high risk of fetal cardiac abnormality — FHx/personal Hx, NT >3.5mm, drugs in pregnancy (lithium), DM, monochorionic twins.

**Other scans**: soft markers (slightly more common in chromosomally abnormal fetuses), fetal growth scans, Doppler US (blood flow, useful in high-risk pregnancies).

### 0.4.1 Work and lifestyle
- Usually safe to continue working unless occupational hazards
- Air travel: avoid after 37w (singletons) / 32w (multiples); associated with ↑VTE risk — wear compression stockings

**Supplementation**
- PO iron: only if anaemic (<105 g/L in 1st trimester or <115 g/L thereafter)
- Vitamin A: high doses (>700 mcg) are teratogenic — avoid eating liver

**Diet advice**: avoid liver and vitamin A; limit caffeine to 200 mg/day (~2 cups tea/coffee); cook meat thoroughly; avoid soft/unpasteurised cheese, shellfish, raw fish.

## 0.5 Aneuploidy & screening

### 0.5.1 Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome)
- 10% die before age 5; life expectancy ~55y
- ↑rate of spontaneous miscarriage
- ↑maternal age → ↑risk (age <25: 1:1500 vs age 45: 1:30)
- Congenital cardiac malformations common (esp. VSD/ASD), duodenal atresia

### 0.5.2 Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome)
- Most die soon after birth; survival >1y rare
- Small chin, low-set ears, rocker-bottom feet, VSD

### 0.5.3 Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome)
- Most die soon after birth
- Microcephaly, holoprosencephaly, exomphalos, cleft lip/palate

### 0.5.4 Screening with combined test
- NT + free hCG + PAPP-A + maternal age
- Performed 11w to 13+6w
- Detects 90% of aneuploidies
- Results: "lower" or "higher chance" — higher chance (>1:150) → counselling

### 0.5.5 Screening with quadruple test
- Used if booking later (15–20w)
- AFP, unconjugated oestriol (UcO), hCG, inhibin A

| Condition | AFP | UcO | hCG | Inhibin A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Down syndrome | ↑ | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |
| Edwards/Patau | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ~ |
| Neural tube defects | ↑ | ~ | ~ | ~ |

### 0.5.6 Non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT)
- Analyses cell-free fetal DNA fragments in maternal blood
- >99% sensitivity/specificity for chromosomal abnormalities, especially Down syndrome
- Private companies offer from 10w gestation

## 0.6 Invasive testing

### 0.6.1 Chorionic villus sampling
- Performed 10–13w; allows earlier termination than amniocentesis
- Placental sampling via transabdominal or transcervical approach
- Risks: ↑miscarriage by 1–2%, ↑BBV transmission, maternal cell contamination, false +ve/-ve
- Not recommended for dichorionic multiple pregnancy

### 0.6.2 Amniocentesis
- Performed from 16w onwards
- Aspiration of fluid containing shed fetal skin/gut cells via transabdominal needle
- Fetal loss rate ~1% at 16w
- Anti-D needed in all Rh-negative women
- Can diagnose fetal infections (e.g. CMV); reduces excess miscarriage rate compared to CVS

## 0.7 Use of anti-D

> [!info] Verified against the National Blood Authority (NBA) Australia's "Guideline for the prophylactic use of Rh D immunoglobulin in pregnancy care" (2024), Aug 2026 — **the dose amounts below needed a genuine correction, not just a units-terminology swap**: Australian dosing is **250 IU for first-trimester events (before 13 weeks)** and **625 IU for events from 13 weeks onward** — the file's original "500 units if ≥20w" understates the correct Australian second/third-trimester dose. The routine antenatal prophylaxis timing (28 and 34 weeks) is genuinely consistent with Australian practice — that part didn't need correcting. Where a larger-volume fetomaternal haemorrhage (FMH) is identified (≥6mL fetal red cells) after 20 weeks, additional dosing is calculated as 100 IU per mL of fetal red cells beyond this threshold, guided by specialist input — a more precise, FMH-volume-based top-up principle than a single flat dose, worth knowing given the original note doesn't capture this. Two RhD immunoglobulin products are available in Australia (CSL Rh(D) Immunoglobulin-VF as 250 IU/625 IU vials, and Rhophylac 1500 IU) — the higher-volume Rhophylac formulation is used where a larger dose is required intravenously.

**Dosing**
- 250 IU for sensitising events before 13 weeks' gestation
- 625 IU for sensitising events from 13 weeks' gestation onward, and for routine antenatal prophylaxis at 28 and 34 weeks
- Give IM in deltoid (IV or SC if bleeding disorder)
- From 20w, perform Kleihauer test (measures bleed volume via maternal FBC) — do **not** give anti-D if already sensitised

**Postnatal use**: 500 units standard dose.
- Give to all Rh-negative women where baby's blood group cannot be determined, or is unknown 72h post-delivery
- Do Kleihauer test on all eligible for anti-D
- Any mother receiving antenatal anti-D should also receive it postnatally, unless she delivers an Rh-negative baby

**Anti-D in miscarriage (Rh-negative mothers)**
- Give to all having surgical or medical terminations, evacuation of hydatidiform mole, and ectopic pregnancies (unless known anti-D antibodies)
- Give where spontaneous miscarriage requires termination
- Give where spontaneous **complete** miscarriage occurs after 12w
- Threatened miscarriage ≥12w: give if bleeding continues intermittently, then q6w until delivery
- Routine anti-D **not** recommended for threatened miscarriage before 12w

**Anti-D in pregnancy (Rh-negative mothers)**
- Blood sample before 28w to check for antibodies
- **Give 625 IU at 28w and 34w** for routine antenatal prophylaxis
- For a sensitising event: **250 IU before 13 weeks; 625 IU from 13 weeks onward** — from 20w, quantify the fetomaternal haemorrhage with a Kleihauer test, as a large bleed needs additional dosing

## 0.8 Antenatal care timetable

> [!info] Verified against RANZCOG's "Routine antenatal assessment in the absence of pregnancy complications" statement and the Australian Government's Pregnancy Care Guidelines, Aug 2026 — **Australia does not use the UK's rigid "10 visits primip / 7 visits multip" NICE framing**; RANZCOG and the Australian Pregnancy Care Guidelines instead recommend an **individualised visit schedule** based on the woman's specific risk profile, explicitly citing evidence that a reduced-visit schedule (as few as 5 visits) does not worsen maternal/perinatal outcomes and is more cost-effective than a higher fixed count — so the specific "10 vs 7" numbers below should not be treated as the Australian standard. What **is** consistent with Australian practice: the first antenatal visit should occur by around 10 weeks' gestation, and the general content/purpose of each visit stage below (booking bloods, anomaly screening, growth/BP/urine monitoring) broadly matches Australian first-visit content (FBC, blood group and antibody screen, rubella/syphilis/HIV/hepatitis serology, MSU, cervical screening where due). Two genuinely useful Australian-specific additions: **influenza and pertussis vaccination are recommended at 20–32 weeks** (not a UK-specific detail, but worth stating explicitly as it's a core part of the Australian schedule), and sleeping on the side is recommended from 28 weeks given the association between supine sleep position and stillbirth risk in later pregnancy.

**NICE recommendations — UK schedule, retained for reference only** (the Australian timetable is the table below)
- 10 visits in 1st pregnancy if uncomplicated
- 7 visits in subsequent pregnancies if uncomplicated
- No need to be seen by consultant if uncomplicated

| Gestation | Content |
|---|---|
| 8–12w (ideally <10w) | Booking visit; booking bloods + urine culture (asymptomatic bacteriuria) |
| 10–13+6w | Early scan to confirm dates, exclude multiple pregnancy |
| 11–13+6w | Down's syndrome screening incl. nuchal scan |
| 16w | Discuss anomaly/blood results; if Hb <110g/L, consider iron; BP + urine dipstick |
| 18–20+6w | Anomaly scan |
| 25w (primip) | BP, urine dipstick, symphysis-fundal height |
| 28w | 2nd anaemia/atypical red cell antibody screen (iron if Hb <105g/L); 1st anti-D dose to Rh-ve women |
| 31w (primip) | Routine care |
| 34w | 2nd anti-D dose; info on labour/birth plan |
| 36w | Check presentation, offer ECV if indicated; breastfeeding/vitamin K/baby blues info |
| 38w | Routine care |
| 40w (primip) | Discuss options for prolonged pregnancy |
| 41w | As per 40w visit |

**Offer screening for**: anaemia, bacteriuria, blood group/Rhesus/anti-RBC antibodies, Down syndrome, fetal anomalies, hepatitis B, HIV, neural tube defects, pre-eclampsia risk factors, syphilis.

**Screen depending on history**: placenta praevia, psychiatric illness, sickle cell disease, Tay-Sachs disease, thalassaemia.

**Do NOT offer routinely**: bacterial vaginosis, chlamydia, CMV, fragile X, hepatitis C, toxoplasmosis.

Listing GBS as "not routinely offered" states one of the two permitted strategies as though it were the national position, and it is the strategy that misses the colonised woman with no risk factors.

## 0.10 Hyperemesis gravidarum

**D**: persistent N/V in pregnancy causing weight loss >5%, dehydration, and electrolyte imbalance.

**R**: ↑hCG (multiple pregnancy, trophoblastic disease), nulliparity, obesity, FHx/PMH of HG. Smoking **↓**risk of HG.

**Criteria for diagnosis**
- ≥5% pre-pregnancy weight loss
- Dehydration
- Electrolyte imbalance

> [!info] PUQE score
> Pregnancy-Unique Quantification of Emesis (PUQE) score used to classify severity of HG.

**S/smx for admission/referral**
- Continued N/V, unable to keep down liquids or oral antiemetics
- Continued N/V with ketonuria and/or weight loss >5% despite oral antiemetics
- Confirmed/suspected comorbidity (e.g. unable to tolerate oral abx for a UTI)

**Mx**
- Simple measures: rest, avoid triggers, bland foods, ginger, wrist acupuncture

> [!info] Verified against Australian Prescriber's "Treatment of nausea and vomiting in pregnancy" and RANZCOG prescribing survey data, Aug 2026 — **two genuinely important, non-obvious Australian-specific points, not just drug-name swaps**: first, the fixed-dose doxylamine/pyridoxine *combination* product (Debendox) was actually **voluntarily withdrawn in Australia in 1983** after birth defect claims (later shown unfounded) — the opposite of the original note's framing, which implied it was an available Australian option "not available in UK." Doxylamine and pyridoxine remain usable in Australia, just as **separate products** (doxylamine as Restavit; pyridoxine separately) rather than a single combination tablet. Second, **real-world Australian prescribing practice diverges from most international guidelines**: RANZCOG survey data shows Australian clinicians overwhelmingly favour **metoclopramide as first-line** for both morning sickness and hyperemesis (70–89% of respondents), with ondansetron commonly used second — a genuinely different practice pattern from UK/US/Canadian guidelines, which place metoclopramide later in the sequence. Ondansetron's small first-trimester cleft lip/palate signal, and the >5-day extrapyramidal-risk caution for metoclopramide/domperidone, both remain accurate and relevant in the Australian context.
> - 1st line (per actual Australian prescribing patterns): metoclopramide, or pyridoxine ± separately-dosed doxylamine (not as a combination product); prochlorperazine or chlorpromazine also used
> - 2nd line: ondansetron (1st trimester use associated with a small increased risk of cleft lip/palate) — commonly used in Australian practice for hyperemesis despite not being formally first-line in most guidelines; metoclopramide or domperidone (**do not use >5 days** — extrapyramidal side effects)

**Ix**: urine dip for ketones/UTI; FBC, U&Es, LFTs; if refractory — TFTs, LFTs, calcium, phosphate, amylase (r/o pancreatitis), ABG; US to diagnose multiple pregnancy and rule out trophoblastic disease.

**Admission Mx**
- IVF: normal saline + K or Hartmann's for rehydration

> [!danger] Do NOT give glucose — can precipitate Wernicke's encephalopathy.

- If vomiting intractable, consider steroids (prednisolone 40–50mg OD or hydrocortisone 100mg/12h IV)
- Prescribe high-dose folic acid 5mg/day, thiamine to prevent Wernicke's
- In hospital: enoxaparin 40mg OD + anti-VTE stockings (dehydration ↑VTE risk)

**Complications**: AKI, Wernicke's encephalopathy, oesophagitis/Mallory-Weiss tear, VTE. Fetal: small ↑ in preterm birth and low birthweight.

## 0.11 Thromboprophylaxis & VTE

*See also →01.07 for general VTE.*

**E**: 0.1–0.2% of all pregnancies. DVT 3x more common than PE. Untreated DVT → PE in 16%. DVT more common postnatally.

**General advice**: avoid immobility and dehydration. Risk ↑ from early 1st trimester to 6w postpartum.

### 0.11.1 Risk stratification

> [!info] VTE risk factors
> **High risk → antenatal LMWH**
> - Hx of ≥2 VTE
> - Unprovoked or oestrogen-related VTE
> - Single provoked VTE + thrombophilia or FHx
> - Antithrombin III deficiency (30% risk)
>
> **Intermediate risk → consider LMWH**
> - Thrombophilia but no VTE
> - Single provoked VTE
> - Medical comorbidities (cancer, inflammatory conditions, significant cardiac/respiratory disease, SLE, sickle cell disease, nephrotic syndrome, IVDU, any antenatal surgery)
>
> **Other risk factors (if ≥3, consider LMWH)**
> - Age >35, obesity, parity ≥3, smoker, large varicose veins, current infection, pre-eclampsia, immobility, dehydration, multiple pregnancy, assisted reproduction
>
> **Postpartum-specific risk factors**
> - Mid-cavity or rotational instrumental delivery
> - PPH + blood transfusion

**Indications**: as per risk factors above. If antenatal LMWH given, continue until 6w postpartum. Emergency LSCS: 7 days postpartum.

> [!info] Verified against the Queensland Clinical Guideline "VTE prophylaxis in pregnancy and the puerperium" (2025), Aug 2026 — **the weight-banded enoxaparin doses below already match current Australian standard prophylactic dosing exactly** (40mg for 50–90kg, 60mg for 91–130kg, 80mg for 131–170kg) — no correction needed to these bands; Australian guidance additionally specifies 20mg daily for <50kg and 0.5mg/kg/day for ≥171kg, extending the range at both ends. **The neuraxial timing needed more precision than a single "4h" figure**: the correct Australian intervals are **12 hours from the last prophylactic LMWH dose to neuraxial block**, **6 hours from neuraxial block back to the next LMWH dose**, and **4 hours from epidural catheter removal to the next dose** — three genuinely different intervals depending on the specific step, not one blanket figure.
> - Enoxaparin: 20mg SC OD if <50kg; 40mg if 50–90kg; 60mg if 91–130kg; 80mg if 131–170kg; 0.5mg/kg if ≥171kg
> - 12h from last LMWH dose to neuraxial block; 6h from neuraxial block to next dose; 4h from catheter removal to next dose
> - **Do not give if PPH ongoing**
> - Otherwise can give immediately

**Ix**: FBC, U&Es, LFTs, clotting. If PE suspected: ABG, ECG, CXR. D-dimer only useful if negative (to rule out) — don't routinely order.

**Imaging**
- DVT: compression or duplex US of deep veins
- High clinical suspicion: continue treatment dose LMWH + repeat imaging in 1w
- PE: CXR → (if normal) US of deep veins → if still negative, V/Q scan ± spiral CT/MRI

**Mx — start ASAP if suspected**

> [!danger] Massive PE
> Immediate senior help. Thrombolysis or percutaneous catheter thrombus fragmentation. Consider UFH. Take blood for APTT 6h post-loading dose; aim for 1.5–2.5x lab control value.

- Generally: LMWH (BD dosing per local formulary), refer to haematology for follow-up, consider switching to warfarin post-delivery. Continue for ≥6w postpartum.

**During labour**
- Stop LMWH; keep well hydrated
- Avoid regional anaesthesia until ≥12h after last prophylactic LMWH dose, or ≥24h after treatment dose
- Wait >4h until epidural catheter removed before next dose
- Do not remove catheter until >12h after last dose
- High risk patients stopping anticoagulation: consider UFH ± IVC filter

## 0.12 Pre-existing problems in pregnancy

### 0.12.1 Anaemia in pregnancy

- Defined as Hb <105 g/L
- Antenatal screening at booking + 28w

> [!info] Verified against the Australian Government Pregnancy Care Guideline — **the thresholds below already match current Australian practice exactly**: Hb <110g/L before 20 weeks, <105g/L at or after 20 weeks — no correction needed. (The postpartum <100g/L threshold reflects a separate, additional convention some sources use and remains reasonable, though it's worth noting the core Australian antenatal guideline defines the antenatal thresholds specifically by the 20-week mark rather than by trimester as originally framed here.)
> - Before 20 weeks: <110 g/L
> - At or after 20 weeks: <105 g/L
> - Postpartum: <100 g/L

- Offer PO ferrous sulfate 200mg BD with orange juice; if intolerant, alternate days or 2x/week. Avoid with tea, calcium, metal-containing supplements. Continue for 3 months after correction to replenish stores.
- Thalassaemia: refer to specialists
- Sickle cell: aim for diagnosis at birth (cord blood) for penicillin pneumococcal prophylaxis

### 0.12.2 Diabetes in pregnancy

DM complicates 5% of pregnancies: 87.5% GDM, 7.5% T1DM, 5% T2DM.

**R**: BMI >30, previous macrosomia (≥4.5kg), previous GDM, 1st-degree FHx, ethnicity.

**Screening (OGTT)**
- Previous GDM: OGTT ASAP after booking, and at 24–28w
- Other risk factors: OGTT within 24–28w
- NICE recommends early self-monitoring of blood glucose *(UK NICE guidance — **not verified against an Australian source**; see `PENDING_GUIDELINE_CHECKS.md` B32)*

**Dx**: fasting glucose ≥5.6 mmol/L OR 2h-OGTT ≥7.8 mmol/L

**Mx of newly diagnosed GDM**
- Joint diabetes-antenatal clinic within 1 week
- Teach self-monitoring of BGC; advise diet/exercise
- If fasting glucose <7: trial diet and exercise; if targets not met within 1–2w, start metformin; if still not met, add insulin
- If fasting glucose ≥7 at diagnosis: start short-acting insulin
- If glucose 6–6.9 with evidence of complications: start insulin

**Mx of pre-existing DM**
- Weight loss if BMI >27
- Stop hypoglycaemics except metformin, start insulin (tight control ↓complications)
- Folic acid 5mg/day pre-conception to 12w
- Detailed anomaly scan at 20w including 4-chamber cardiac view + outflow tracts
- Treat retinopathy (can worsen in pregnancy)

**C**: 50% of women with GDM develop T2DM — check fasting glucose 6w postpartum or HbA1c at 13w postpartum; screen annually thereafter.

### 0.12.3 Hypertension in pregnancy

**Normal BP course**: ↓ in early pregnancy until ~24w, then ↑ thereafter (↑stroke volume).

> [!danger] BP >160/110 = EMERGENCY

- HTN before 20w or high booking BP (>130/80) → likely to develop chronic HTN.

**Chronic hypertension**: 3–5% of pregnancies. Risks: pre-eclampsia, fetal growth restriction, placental abruption.

> [!danger] ACE inhibitors/ARBs are teratogenic — must be changed pre-conception to labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine, or methyldopa.

- **Antenatal Mx**: aim BP <150/90 (DBP ≥80); aspirin 75mg OD until birth; admit if >160/110; fetal US q4w from 28w (growth, amniotic fluid, umbilical artery Dopplers); CTG if fetal activity abnormal; aim to induce around EDD
- **Intrapartum Mx**: monitor BP hourly if <159/109, continuously if ≥160/110; if refractory, advise operative delivery

> [!danger] Ergometrine contraindicated — give oxytocin alone at 3rd stage of labour.

- **Postnatal Mx**: check BP daily days 1–5, and at 2w; change methyldopa to alternative antihypertensive (postnatal depression risk); avoid diuretics if breastfeeding

**Pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH)**: HTN (>140/90) in 2nd half of pregnancy without proteinuria or other pre-eclampsia features. 6–7% of pregnancies.

- **Antenatal Mx**: urine testing/ACR to rule out pre-eclampsia; mild → weekly urine/BP + 4-weekly growth scans; >150/100 → start tx + check 2x/week; ≥160/110 → admit, BP every 6h, urine OD, FBC/U&Es/AST/ALT/bilirubin at presentation and weekly; aim delivery after 37w unless pre-eclampsia develops
- **Intrapartum Mx**: continue antihypertensives; monitor BP hourly, continuously if >160/110 (advise C-section)
- **Postnatal Mx**: continue tx until BP <130/80; review at 2w and 6w; refer to cardiology if still needed at 6w

### 0.12.4 Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy

- Usually Graves' disease; associated with infertility, prematurity, fetal loss, malformations
- Managed with PTU (↓placental crossing); partial thyroidectomy if severe; monitor ≥monthly
- TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies can cause fetal thyrotoxicosis (1%) after 24w — test TFTs frequently in affected babies; usually resolves spontaneously in 2–3 months
- Thyroid storm can be precipitated by labour — urgent treatment required

### 0.12.5 Jaundice in pregnancy

- Occurs in 1:1500 pregnancies; diagnose urgently (travel Hx, drug Hx, BBV)

**Obstetric cholestasis** (0.7% prevalence)
- Pruritus (palms, soles) in 2nd half, no rash, worse at night
- Diagnosis of exclusion — rule out BBV, autoimmune disease, US of liver
- Weekly LFTs; give vitamin K 10mg/24h if abnormal clotting screen, 1mg to baby at birth
- Typically induced at 37w; ursodeoxycholic acid ↓itch
- Symptoms resolve within 1w of delivery; can recur with COCP and in ≤70% subsequent pregnancies

**Acute fatty liver of pregnancy**
- Rare but serious; abdominal pain, jaundice, headache, vomiting ± thrombocytopenia, pancreatitis
- Associated with pre-eclampsia in 30–60%
- Complications: coma, death
- Mx in HDU/ICU — supportive; expedite delivery

> [!danger] Epidural/regional anaesthesia contraindicated in acute fatty liver of pregnancy.

**Other causes of jaundice**: viral, severe pre-eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, anaesthesia (halothane).

### 0.12.6 Malaria in pregnancy

**S/smx**: consider in any woman presenting with odd behaviour, fever, jaundice, sweating, DIC, fetal distress, premature labour, seizures, or loss of consciousness → do thick and thin films, seek expert help.

- Advise against travel to endemic areas, or use of preventative measures (mosquito nets, insect repellent).

### 0.12.7 Renal disease in pregnancy

**Asymptomatic bacteriuria**
- 2% of sexually active women; ≤7% of pregnant women (↑ in DM, renal transplant); 30% progress to pyelonephritis

> [!info] Verified against SA Health's "Urinary Tract Infections in Pregnancy" guideline — the drug choices below (cefalexin, trimethoprim, nitrofurantoin) are consistent with Australian practice; the nitrofurantoin avoidance threshold is more precisely **after 37 weeks' gestation** (or sooner if early birth is planned) rather than a vaguer "3rd trimester," given the specific concern is proximity to delivery (theoretical neonatal haemolysis risk), not the trimester itself.
> If present on MSU: cefalexin 500mg TDS PO for 5 days; trimethoprim (avoid in 1st trimester); nitrofurantoin (avoid after 37 weeks' gestation or if early birth is planned).

**Pyelonephritis**
- 1–2% of pregnant women; do blood and urine culture

> [!info] Verified against SA Health's "Urinary Tract Infections in Pregnancy" guideline — admission for a minimum of 48 hours of IV antibiotic therapy is consistent with Australian practice; IV-to-oral switch once afebrile for ≥24h, inflammatory markers settling, and clinically improving, is the correct transition principle. The specific IV agent should be guided by local antibiogram/sensitivities and current eTG rather than assumed fixed — cefuroxime is a reasonable option but check current local guidance for the preferred first-line agent given this varies more by local resistance patterns than by a single national standard.
> IV abx per local guidance (e.g. cefuroxime or similar) ± stat-dose gentamicin depending on severity. Minimum 48h IV therapy, then switch to PO once afebrile ≥24h and clinically improving; total course 2–3w. Repeat MSU to confirm eradication. If ≥2 confirmed UTIs in pregnancy: renal US, consider abx prophylaxis for remainder of pregnancy.

**AKI related to pregnancy**
- Causes: sepsis, haemolysis (e.g. HELLP), hypovolaemia, volume contraction (e.g. pre-eclampsia), drugs (esp. NSAIDs)
- Monitor urine output and fluid balance (catheterisation), aim >30 mL/h + renal bloods; refer to specialist if needed

### 0.12.8 Psychiatric conditions — drugs in pregnancy

- Risk vs benefit; consult specialists especially in severe depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia
- High risk of rapid postpartum relapse with significant risks to mother and infant, especially in bipolar disorder (up to 50%)

**Antidepressants**
- SSRIs, usually sertraline: small risk of persistent fetal pulmonary HTN and neonatal withdrawal
- Avoid paroxetine
- Breastfeeding OK unless detrimental to maternal wellbeing

> [!danger] Mood stabilisers (AEDs)
> **Valproate and carbamazepine are contraindicated in women of childbearing age (WoCA).**
> - Valproate: 10% malformation rate (NTDs, craniofacial abnormalities), 30–40% neurodevelopmental problems
> - Carbamazepine: 2.2% malformation rate
> - Lamotrigine: 2.1% malformation rate
> - Avoid all in breastfeeding also

**Lithium**: associated with teratogenicity, neonatal thyroid abnormality, floppy baby syndrome.
- Only prescribe if no other choice; offer specialist fetal echo
- Monitor drug levels q4w to 36w, then weekly; do not change brands
- Signs of toxicity: tremor, drowsiness, visual disturbances
- Stop during labour; check 12h post-dose level, restart based on result
- **No breastfeeding** — neonatal toxicity risk

**Antipsychotics**
- Rates of fetal abnormality ↑ in schizophrenia even in untreated women
- Women on antipsychotics with weight gain should have OGTT
- **Clozapine**: do not breastfeed (fetal agranulocytosis risk)
- Avoid depot medications in WoCA

**Benzodiazepines**: associated with cleft lip/palate — avoid. Avoid diazepam in delivery (neonatal withdrawal risk).

### 0.12.9 Epilepsy in pregnancy

**D**: ~0.5% of women of childbearing age. Other seizure causes to exclude: eclampsia, cerebral vein thrombosis, intracranial mass, stroke, metabolic abnormality, drugs/withdrawal.

**Before pregnancy**
- Optimise treatment to lowest effective dose
- Folic acid 5mg for >3 months prior to conception
- Counsel on ↑risk of epilepsy in children (4–5% if either parent affected, 15–20% if both)
- Drug-specific notes:
  - Lamotrigine: possibly lower malformation rates than other AEDs; may need dose ↑
  - Valproate: associated with neural tube defects
  - Carbamazepine: considered least teratogenic of older AEDs
  - Phenytoin: associated with cleft palate

**During pregnancy**
- Consultant-led clinic, aim for vaginal delivery
- Nuchal/anomaly scans (± fetal echo), serial growth scans in 3rd trimester (↑SGA risk)
- 5mg folic acid daily for 1st trimester
- Routine drug level testing not recommended
- Avoid stress and sleep deprivation (↑seizure risk)

**Intrapartum**: deliver in hospital, continue AEDs; epidural safe for pain relief; benzodiazepines if seizure not self-terminating (e.g. lorazepam 4mg IV). Seizures more common intra/postpartum due to ↓sleep.

**Postpartum**
- Vitamin K 1mg to baby
- Inpatient for 24h postpartum (↑risk)
- Advise strategies to avoid dropping baby if seizure occurs (e.g. changing baby on floor)
- Encourage breastfeeding; review AED dose within 10 days of delivery to avoid toxicity
- Discuss contraception — oestrogen-containing methods contraindicated; ↑seizure risk with lamotrigine interactions

### 0.12.10 Connective tissue disease in pregnancy

**Rheumatoid arthritis**
- 75% improve during pregnancy, but 90% flare in puerperium
- Medications: MTX unsafe (stop ≥6 months before conception); leflunomide unsafe; sulfasalazine/HCQ safe; TNF-α blockers possibly safe; low-dose corticosteroids may control symptoms; paracetamol for pain; NSAIDs OK until 32w (risk of early ductus arteriosus closure)
- Refer to obstetric anaesthetist (risk of atlanto-axial subluxation)

**SLE**
- Flares more common in pregnancy and puerperium — can ↑risk to baby
- Planned pregnancies in remission have better outcomes
- Flares managed with corticosteroids + HCQ
- Aspirin 75–150mg from +ve pregnancy test until delivery
- If mother Ro+ve: 2% risk of congenital heart block, 5% transient neonatal cutaneous lupus

**Antiphospholipid syndrome**
- Untreated: <20% pregnancies proceed to live birth (1st trimester loss or placental thrombosis)
- ↑risk of pre-eclampsia and IUGR
- Mx: uterine artery Dopplers at 20–24w; if high risk, 4-weekly growth scans + umbilical artery Dopplers from 28w; aspirin 75–150mg OD if no previous adverse outcome (70–80% live birth rate); if previous fetal loss, add LMWH (enoxaparin 40mg SC) from fetal heart identification (~6w) throughout pregnancy; postpartum: heparin or warfarin

### 0.12.11 HIV in pregnancy

- Without intervention, 15% of babies acquire HIV from an HIV+ve mother
- 2/3 of vertical transmission occurs during pregnancy; breastfeeding doubles the rate
- ↑risk with: longer membrane rupture, viral load >400, seroconversion during pregnancy, advanced disease, pre-term labour, HCV co-infection
- ≤1% risk with maternal HAART, elective C-section, bottle feeding
  - Vaginal delivery recommended if viral load <50 at 36w, otherwise C-section
  - Start zidovudine infusion 4h before C-section
- Screening at booking and 28w; if status unknown at labour, perform rapid test and manage accordingly

### 0.12.12 Rubella in pregnancy

**Maternal s/smx** — absent in 50%: fever prodrome, maculopapular rash starting on face spreading to body, lymphadenopathy.

- Fetus most at risk in first 16w; 80% of fetuses affected if maternal primary infection in first 12w
- Miscarriage or stillbirth may occur

**Congenital rubella syndrome**: sensorineural deafness, congenital cataracts (infection at 8–9w), congenital heart disease e.g. PDA (infection at 5–10w), growth retardation, hepatosplenomegaly, purpuric skin lesions, 'salt and pepper' chorioretinitis, microphthalmia, cerebral palsy.

**Ix**: antibodies 10 days apart; look for IgM 4–5w from incubation period or contact date. If confirmed in 1st trimester, termination offered without invasive prenatal diagnosis.

### 0.12.13 Measles in pregnancy

**Maternal s/smx**: prodromal irritability, cough, conjunctivitis, fever; Koplik spots (before rash); rash starts behind ears, spreads to whole body (discrete maculopapular → blotchy/confluent); diarrhoea in 10%.

**Ix**: IgM antibodies detected >4 days and <1 month of rash onset; viral RNA in saliva.

**Implications in pregnancy**: associated with fetal loss and preterm delivery; no congenital infection/abnormality syndrome. If maternal rash appears 6 days pre- or post-delivery, give human normal immunoglobulin immediately after birth/exposure to prevent neonatal subacute sclerosing panencephalitis.

**Complications**: encephalitis (typically 1–2w post-illness); subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (very rare, may present 5–10y after illness — behavioural change, myoclonus, choreoathetosis, dystonia, dementia, coma, death); febrile convulsions.
