## Congenital diaphragmatic hernia

- **D:** protrusion of abdominal contents into the thoracic cavity due to a defect in the diaphragm. Incidence 1:2400 — only 50% survival.
- **S/Smx:** usually diagnosed prenatally by ultrasound. On birth: respiratory distress, cyanosis, ↓breath sounds + bowel sounds in the hemithorax, displaced heart sounds, scaphoid abdomen. Small hernias may present later with minor problems. 61% have other abnormalities.
- **Dx/Ix:** prenatal ultrasound, postnatal CXR.
- **Mx:**
  - Prenatal: fetal tracheal obstruction by balloon, which encourages lung growth and pushes out viscera.
  - Postnatal: intubate, ventilate, paralyse with minimal pressures.

> [!danger] DO NOT use facemask ventilation, as air may enter the gut.

  - Surgery in a tertiary centre.

## Inguinal hernia

- **D:** protrusion of abdominal or pelvic contents through a dilated internal inguinal ring or attenuated inguinal floor into the inguinal canal.
- **S/Smx:** bulge lateral to the pubic tubercle when abdominal pressure increases (e.g. crying). R>L. M>F 5:1.

> [!tip] Important DDx: hydrocele — similar aetiology, but only allows fluid from the peritoneal cavity to pass through. Generally closes during the 1st year of life. If it persists after 2 years, may need surgical closure.

- **Mx — "6-2 rule":**
  - Baby <6 weeks: repair within 2 days (greatest risk of strangulation).
  - <6 months: repair within 2 weeks.
  - <6 years: repair within 2 months.

## Imperforate anus

- **D:** congenital anorectal malformation where a normal anal opening is absent at birth. 1 in 5000 births in the US. M>F slightly. Associated with some specific syndromes, trisomies, and other abnormalities.
- **S/Smx:** failure to pass meconium within 24h. Meconium in urine — girls may have a fourchette fistula; boys may have a posterior urethral fistula.
- **Dx/Ix:** GU imaging to find other anomalies.
- **Mx:** posterior sagittal anorectoplasty.

## Mid-gut malrotation

- **D:** spectrum of rotational and fixation disturbances that can occur during embryonic development.
- **A:** arrested rotation → abnormal intestinal fixations + malrotation.
- **P:** malrotation increases susceptibility to volvulus, ischaemia, necrosis.
- **S/Smx of volvulus (3–7 days after birth):** bilious neonatal vomiting → immediate surgical referral (+NGT). Also epigastric distention, pain, associated with abnormalities of adjacent organs; ± feeding difficulties.

> [!danger] Blood PR (possible mid-gut necrosis) → emergency surgical decompression.

- **Dx/Ix:** UGI contrast study — the DJ flexure is more medially placed. Ultrasound may show abnormal orientation of the SMA and SMV.
- **Mx:** Ladd's procedure.

## Gastroschisis and omphalocele

- **D:** congenital defects of the abdominal wall resulting in intestinal herniation from the abdominal cavity.
  - **Gastroschisis** (1 in 3000): para-umbilical defect with evisceration of abdominal contents.
  - **Omphalocele:** defect of the umbilical ring with herniation of abdominal viscera, covered by peritoneum and amniotic membrane. Major >4cm, minor <4cm. 72% associated with other malformations. Associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome, Down syndrome.
- **R:** maternal age <20 (gastroschisis) or >35 (omphalocele), M>F, smoking.
- **Dx/Ix:** antenatal ultrasound.
- **Mx of gastroschisis:** vaginal delivery may be attempted. Surgical emergency! At delivery, cover exposed bowel in cling film, avoid heat and fluid loss. Theatre ASAP (within 4h). TPN may be required for a while.
- **Mx of omphalocele:** C-section to reduce risk of sac rupture. Staged repair if needed — allows the sac to granulate and epithelialise over coming weeks; as the infant grows, the sac contents will be able to fit within the abdominal cavity, then the shell is removed and the abdomen closed.

## Meckel's diverticulum

- **D:** congenital diverticulum of the small intestine — contains mucosa.

> [!tip] "Rule of 2s": occurs in 2% of the population; 2% are symptomatic; 2× more common in males; 2 feet from the ileocaecal valve; 2 inches long.

- **S/Smx:** generally symptomatic when the patient is younger. Painless rectal bleeding ("currant jelly" coloured stool) in children, or melaena in adults. Abdominal pain — can present similarly to appendicitis.
- **Ix:** "Meckel's scan" (usually in kids) — technetium scan, or mesenteric arteriography in more severe cases.
- **Mx:** if active bleeding + large blood loss, may need blood transfusion. Definitively, resection of the diverticulum.
