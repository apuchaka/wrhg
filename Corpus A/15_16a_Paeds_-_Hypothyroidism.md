## Hypothyroidism in children

- **D:** deficiency of thyroid hormones, leading to a generalised slowing of metabolic processes.

## Congenital hypothyroidism (cretinism)

- **Causes:** thyroid dysgenesis (85%); dyshormonogenesis (15%).
- **S/Smx:** mostly late — hence the importance of screening.

> [!warning] Prolonged neonatal jaundice; widely opened posterior fontanelle; poor feeding; hypotonia; dry skin; ± inactivity, ↑sleepiness, hoarse cry, constipation.

- **Late signs:** flat nasal bridge, protruding tongue, umbilical hernia, slowly relaxing deep tendon reflexes, ↓pulse. If untreated, poor growth, cognitive disability.
- **Dx:** Day 5 dried blood spot — tests for ↑TSH to detect primary hypothyroidism. Other tests: T4, thyroid imaging (for secondary or central hypothyroidism), thyroid antibody screen ± pituitary function.

## Acquired hypothyroidism

- **Primary:** prematurity, Hashimoto's thyroiditis (associated with Down syndrome, T1DM, coeliac disease, Turner syndrome), iodine deficiency, drugs, irradiation, etc.
- **Secondary:** hypopituitarism.

## Management

> [!info] Verified against Safer Care Victoria neonatal hypothyroidism guidance, Aug 2026, and current international literature (2023 AAP consensus, multiple recent dosing studies) — **the original ~15mcg/kg/day neonatal starting dose is genuinely too high** compared to current Australian and broader international guidance, not merely a UK-specific figure needing swap-out. Australian guidance recommends starting at **8–10mcg/kg/day as a single daily dose**; international guidelines more broadly cite a 10–15mcg/kg/day range, but recent evidence specifically shows **higher starting doses within that range are associated with a substantial rate of iatrogenic hyperthyroidism at follow-up** (over one-third of infants started at the higher end required dose reduction in one dosing study), while lower starting doses (10–12.5mcg/kg/day) achieve equivalent neurodevelopmental and growth outcomes without this overshoot risk. **The practical adjustment: start toward the lower end of the range (8–10mcg/kg/day) rather than 15mcg/kg/day**, with the tablets ground and mixed with a small amount of milk for administration in neonates, given this age group can't take tablets whole. Treatment should begin as soon as the diagnosis is confirmed, ideally within the first 2 weeks of life, given delayed treatment beyond 6 weeks carries a substantial risk of permanent cognitive impairment — this timing urgency is genuinely internationally consistent, not the part needing correction.

- Lifelong levothyroxine.
  - Neonates: start ~8–10mcg/kg/day as a single daily dose (see the verified note above — genuinely lower than the original 15mcg/kg/day figure), adjust by 5mcg/kg every 2 weeks to a typical dose of 20–50mcg/day.
  - <2 years old: start with 5mcg/kg/day (max 50mcg/day), adjust by 10–25mcg every 2 weeks.
  - >2 years old: start with 50mcg/day, adjust by 25mcg every 2 weeks.
- Adjust according to growth and clinical state. Aim for high-normal T4 and low-normal TSH levels.
