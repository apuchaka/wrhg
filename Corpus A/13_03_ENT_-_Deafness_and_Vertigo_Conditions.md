## Childhood deafness

- 1 in 500 newborns has bilateral permanent SNHL ≥40dB.
- Divided: 50% genetic, 25% non-genetic, 25% idiopathic.

> [!info] Non-genetic causes (25%)
> - Intrauterine TORCH infection — e.g. CMV, rubella, toxoplasmosis, HSV, syphilis.
> - Perinatal — prematurity, hypoxia, intraventricular haemorrhage, kernicterus.
> - Infections — meningitis, encephalitis, measles, mumps.
> - Others — ototoxic drugs, acoustic or cranial trauma.

- **Universal newborn hearing screening** — within weeks of birth: otoacoustic emissions, auditory brainstem responses.
- **Subjective hearing tests in older children:** distraction testing (6–18 months); visual reinforced audiometry (6–30 months); speech discrimination (24–60 months).
- **Mx:** support, advice, information. Hearing aids or cochlear implants (a multichannel electrode inserted surgically into the cochlea that directly stimulates the auditory nerve to produce electrical signals).

## Deafness in adults

> [!danger] Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is defined as loss of ≥30dB, in 3 contiguous pure tone frequencies, over 3 days.

- **Ix/Mx:** immediate specialist referral. Work-up includes MRI (to rule out acoustic neuroma); otherwise diagnosed as idiopathic sudden SNHL. High-dose steroids (prednisolone 60mg OD for 7 days, then 7 days tapering).
- **P:** negative prognostic factors include age <15 or >65, ↑ESR, vertigo, hearing loss in the opposite ear, severe hearing loss.
- Conductive hearing loss — a cause is "always" found: infection, occlusion, trauma, fracture, etc.

## Otosclerosis

- **D:** abnormal bone remodelling in the middle ear, where bone tissue is replaced by vascular spongy bone.
- **A:** unknown; possibly autosomal dominant; 50% family history, 85% bilateral, F>M (2:1).
- **P:** spongy bone forms around the stapes footplate. Calcification leads to fixation → progressive conductive hearing loss.
- **S/Smx:** starts in early adult life, may be accelerated by pregnancy. ~75% have tinnitus ± vertigo. 10% have Schwartze's sign (flamingo pink blush in the posterior segment). Audiometry: Carhart's notch.
- **Mx:** watch and wait, hearing aid, or surgery to replace the fixed stapes/cochlear.

> [!warning] Careful patient selection for surgery is needed, as it can be complicated by complete SNHL — surgery only on the worse hearing ear; contralateral SNHL is a contraindication.

## Vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma)

- **D:** benign cerebellopontine angle tumour that grows from the superior vestibular component of the vestibulocochlear nerve.
- **R:** neurofibromatosis type 2.
- **S/Smx according to affected cranial nerve:**
  - CN VIII: vertigo or balance problems (20%), unilateral SNHL (70%), unilateral tinnitus (50%).
  - CN V: absent corneal reflex.
  - CN VII: facial palsy.
  - Bilateral in NF type 2.
  - Larger tumours: mass effect (e.g. ↑ICP).
- **Ix:** urgent referral to ENT; MRI of the cerebellopontine angle; audiometry.
- **DDx:** meningioma (10%), epidermoid tumours (5%).
- **Mx:** watch and wait with interval MRI scans for small, slow-growing tumours. Surgical removal for large or rapidly growing tumours; stereotactic radiosurgery.

## Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)

- **D:** peripheral vestibular disorder that manifests as sudden, short-lived episodes of vertigo elicited by specific head movements.
- **R:** ↑age, F>M, head trauma, vestibular neuronitis or labyrinthitis, migraines, inner ear surgery, Ménière's disease.
- **S/Smx:** vertigo triggered by change in head position ± nausea; episodes last 10–20 seconds.
- **Dx:** Dix-Hallpike manoeuvre — vertigo and rotatory nystagmus.
- **Mx:** watch and wait — self-resolving in weeks to months usually. Epley manoeuvre; Brandt-Daroff exercises. Medications (e.g. betahistine) have limited value.

## Ménière's disease

- **D:** disorder of the inner ear of unknown cause, characterised by excessive pressure and progressive dilation of the endolymphatic system.
- **P:** possibly over-production or impaired absorption of endolymph.

> [!info] Why hydrops produces this specific triad, not isolated symptoms — worth connecting explicitly: the membranous labyrinth is a single continuous fluid-filled system linking the vestibular apparatus (semicircular canals, utricle, saccule) and the cochlea. Endolymphatic hydrops distends this shared system, so a single pathological process affects both structures simultaneously — vestibular distension produces the vertigo, while cochlear distension produces the tinnitus and sensorineural hearing loss. This is precisely why the triad occurs together rather than as isolated, unrelated symptoms, and why early Ménière's disease (before hydrops has affected both structures equally) can present with an incomplete picture before progressing to the full triad.

> [!info] Classic triad — ≥2 episodes of vertigo, tinnitus, and SNHL.
> - Vertigo: lasts 20 minutes to 12 hours ± nausea/vomiting.
> - Tinnitus: described as "roaring".

- **Other features:** aural fullness (sensation of pressure and fullness in the ear); "drop attacks" (sudden loss of balance, but no loss of consciousness); bilateral in 30–50%.
- **Mx:** ENT referral. Inform the driver licensing authority per Austroads' Assessing Fitness to Drive guidelines (the Australian equivalent of the UK's DVLA reporting requirement) — current advice is to cease driving until satisfactory symptom control, given the risk of sudden vertigo/drop attacks while driving.
  - Acute attacks: buccal or IM prochlorperazine ± admission.
  - Prevention: betahistine, vestibular rehabilitation exercises.

## Vestibular neuronitis

- **D:** peripheral disorder of vertigo caused by inflammation of the vestibular nerve.
- **R:** recent viral illness (especially URTI).
- **S/Smx:** vertigo, nausea, vomiting, unsteadiness. NO hearing loss or tinnitus. Nystagmus (fine horizontal). ± positive head impulse test.
- **Ix:** clinical diagnosis.
- **Mx:** advice on safe driving + working. If severe symptoms — cyclizine, prochlorperazine, cinnarizine, or promethazine (PO usually sufficient; IM or buccal if desired).

> [!warning] Short course only — otherwise the patient may get rebound symptoms.

- Usually self-limiting (4–6 weeks). Chronic illness: vestibular rehabilitation exercises.

## (Viral) labyrinthitis

- **D:** peripheral disorder of vertigo and hearing loss caused by inflammation of the otic capsule (the labyrinth).
- **R:** viral URTI, chronic suppurative otitis media, acute otitis media, cholesteatoma.
- **S/Smx:** vertigo, dizziness, nausea/vomiting; nystagmus (fine horizontal); hearing loss (may be uni- or bilateral); tinnitus; ± positive head impulse test.
- **Ix:** clinical diagnosis.
- **Mx:** same as vestibular neuronitis.
