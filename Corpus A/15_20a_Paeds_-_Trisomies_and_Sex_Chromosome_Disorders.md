## Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)

- **D:** trisomy 21. 1:800 births.
- **R:** ↑maternal age (age 20 → 1:1500 risk; age 40 → 1:100 risk), previous child with Down syndrome, parental karyotype with translocation.
- **A:** extra chromosome 21. **P:** may be due to non-disjunction of chromosome pairs during gamete formation, Robertsonian translocation, or mosaicism.

> [!info] Features in the neonatal period: hypotonia; poor Moro reflex; hyper-flexibility of joints; extra skin on back of neck; flat facial profile; slanted palpebral fissures; anomalous auricles; hypoplasia of iliac wings; short middle phalanx of the fifth finger; single palmar crease.

### Associated conditions

- **GI:** duodenal atresia, Hirschsprung's disease.
- **Cardiac:** AVSD (40%, most common); VSD (30%); ASD (10%); Tetralogy of Fallot (~5%); isolated PDA (~5%).
- **Vision:** refractive errors more common; strabismus (20–40%); cataracts (congenital or acquired); recurrent blepharitis; glaucoma.
- **Hearing:** otitis media very common.
- **Haematological:** infants may be born with transient abnormal myelopoiesis (TAM) that may progress to ALL or AML.
- **Neurological:** learning difficulties; associated with Alzheimer's disease (onset in early 50s); epilepsy in 8%.
- **Respiratory:** obstructive sleep apnoea; recurrent infections.
- **Thyroid:** hypothyroidism more common.
- **Others:** atlantoaxial instability.

- **Ix:** chromosomal karyotype for diagnosis. Other tests according to complications (e.g. echocardiogram).
- **Mx:** assessment + specialist consultation; parental genetic counselling.
- **P:** average life expectancy 50–60 years old.

## Patau syndrome (Trisomy 13)

- **R:** ↑maternal age. **A/P:** as with Down syndrome; typically causes "midline" defects (e.g. cleft lip/palate).
- **S/Smx:** microcephaly; small or no eyes; cleft lip or palate; polydactyly; scalp lesions; cardiac abnormalities (any); other organ abnormalities. After infancy, patients have severe psychomotor disorder, failure to thrive, intellectual disability, and seizures.
- **P:** median survival 7–10 days; 90% live <1 year.

## Edwards syndrome (Trisomy 18)

- **R:** ↑maternal age.
- **S/Smx:** micrognathia; low-set ears; rocker bottom feet; overlapping of fingers; cardiac defects in 90%; all organ systems may be affected.
- **P:** median survival 3–14 days; 90–95% live <1 year.

## Turner syndrome

- **D:** monosomy 45,XO — absence of the second sex chromosome in phenotypical females. ~1:5000 females.
- **R:** no known risk factors.
- **S/Smx:** abnormal facies (narrow or high arched palate, short/broad/webbed neck, low-set anomalous pinnae). Short stature (usually <5th percentile — rule out other causes of poor growth). Shield chest, widely spaced nipples. Cystic hygroma. Short fourth metacarpal. Multiple pigmented naevi.
- **Cardiac:** bicuspid aortic valve (15%); coarctation of the aorta (5–10%); ↑risk of aortic dilation and dissection.
- **Endocrine:** primary amenorrhoea; ↑gonadotropin levels; hypothyroidism.
- **Renal:** horseshoe kidney.
- Lymphoedema in neonates. ↑risk of autoimmune disease and Crohn's disease.
- May not be diagnosed until the teens, when patients present due to short stature and delayed puberty.
- **Ix:** karyotype for diagnosis.
- **Mx:** surveillance and preventative care. Once the patient has cyclical bleeding — ovarian HRT (oral or transdermal oestrogen therapy, continued until ~50 years old). Education on reproductive issues — most women with Turner syndrome are infertile, but spontaneous menses and pregnancies occur in 2–3%; genetic counselling required.

## Klinefelter syndrome

- **D:** ≥2 X chromosomes in a phenotypical male. 47,XXY is the most common form. ~1:1000 males.
- **R:** ↑maternal & paternal age.
- **S/Smx:** often taller than average. Genital abnormalities — lack of secondary sexual characteristics; small, firm testes (↑gonadotropins & low testosterone); infertility (the most common presentation). Gynaecomastia (associated with ↑breast cancer risk). Intelligence usually in the normal range, but some have educational and psychological problems.

## Triple X syndrome

- **D:** trisomy X — 47,XXX. 1:1000 females.
- **S/Smx:** wide range of phenotypes — estimated that only 10% are clinically diagnosed (the other 90% likely subclinical). At birth: hypotonia, clinodactyly (a finger that curves to one side). Physical features: tall, epicanthal folds, widely spaced eyes (hypertelorism). Other associated conditions: genitourinary malformations, seizure disorder, intention tremor, congenital hip dysplasia, constipation or abdominal pains — may only be diagnosed in the teens due to late menarche, menstrual irregularities, or subfertility.
