## Male subfertility

**Causes**

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Semen abnormality | Idiopathic oligo-astheno-teratozoospermia, testicular cancer, alcohol/smoking/other drugs, varicocele |
| Azoospermia — pretesticular | Anabolic steroid use, Kallmann syndrome |
| Azoospermia — non-obstructive | Cryptorchidism, orchitis, Klinefelter syndrome, chemotherapy |
| Azoospermia — obstructive | Congenital bilateral absence of vas deferens, vasectomy, chlamydia, gonorrhoea |
| Immunological | Anti-sperm antibodies |
| Coital dysfunction | Erectile dysfunction with normal sperm function (some drugs cause ED — e.g. beta-blockers, antidepressants), hypospadias, phimosis, retrograde ejaculation, failure of ejaculation 2° other causes (e.g. multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury) |

**Ix**: detailed history + examination; testicular US; hormones (LH, FSH, testosterone); genetic tests (karyotyping).

**Mx**: lifestyle modification; consider changing medications; multivitamin containing zinc, selenium, and vitamin C, then repeat semen analysis in 3 months; intracytoplasmic sperm injection if refractory to conservative Mx.

## Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)

**D**: spectrum of inflammatory disorders of the upper female genital tract — any combination of endometritis, salpingitis, tubo-ovarian abscess, and pelvic peritonitis.

**R**: prior chlamydia/gonorrhoea infection, young age of onset of sexual activity, unprotected sex with multiple partners, PMH of PID, IUD use (first 3w post-insertion).

**A**: polymicrobial infection — chlamydia, gonorrhoea, and mycoplasma most common.

**P**: cervical infection ascending to the upper genital tract.

**S/smx**: lower abdominal/pelvic pain (constant or intermittent, uni- or bilateral); fever (may be afebrile if chronic); deep dyspareunia; intermenstrual or postcoital bleeding; dysuria; ± irregular menses; vaginal or cervical discharge; cervical excitation.

**Ix**: pregnancy test (rule out ectopic), FBC (↑WBC), high vaginal swab (often -ve), chlamydia/gonorrhoea screen (NAAT/PCR).

> [!info] Verified against the WA Health "Quick guide to STI and BBV management" (2026 edition), already used to verify the STI regimens in [[17_08_Vaginal_Discharge__Urinary_Incontinence__Pelvic_Organ_Prolapse]] — **Australian first-line for sexually acquired PID is ceftriaxone 500mg IM plus doxycycline 100mg PO BD for 14 days plus metronidazole 400mg PO BD for 14 days** — a genuinely different structure from the file's ofloxacin-based option, which is not standard Australian practice. For patients likely non-adherent to the 14-day doxycycline course, azithromycin 1g PO as a further single dose one week later is a reasonable substitution. Begin treatment early given delayed treatment is associated with significantly increased risk of tubal infertility and ectopic pregnancy. On the IUD question: current Australian guidance doesn't mandate removal — this remains a clinical judgement call based on response to treatment, consistent with the original note's "guidelines differ" framing.
> Low threshold for treating. Ceftriaxone 500mg IM (single dose) **plus** doxycycline 100mg PO BD for 14 days **plus** metronidazole 400mg PO BD for 14 days. For likely doxycycline non-adherence, consider replacing with azithromycin 1g PO as a further single dose one week later. Guidelines differ on whether an existing IUD should be removed.

**Complications**
- **Perihepatitis (Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome)**: liver capsule inflammation with "violin-string" adhesions (~10%); S/smx: RUQ pain; Mx: same as PID
- Infertility due to tubal blockage (↑risk with ↑episodes)
- Chronic pelvic pain
- Ectopic pregnancy

## Endometriosis

**D**: presence of endometrial glands and stroma outside the endometrial cavity and uterine musculature (most commonly pelvic peritoneum and ovaries).

**R**: FHx, nulliparity, Müllerian anomalies (in pre-menarcheal girls).

**A/P**: various theories — retrograde menstruation, genetic predisposition, altered immune response.

**S/smx**
- Chronic pelvic pain — cyclical if 2° endometrial tissue responding to menstrual cycle; constant if 2° chronic inflammation adhesions
- Secondary dysmenorrhoea (pain often starts days before menses) — may be debilitating (inability to attend school/work)
- Deep dyspareunia
- Subfertility
- Urinary symptoms: dysuria, urgency, haematuria, painful bowel movements
- Anxiety and depression due to symptoms
- Pelvic exam: ↓organ mobility, tender nodularity in posterior vaginal fornix ± visible vaginal endometrial lesions

**Ix**: laparoscopy is the key investigation — little role for US or other Ix as direct visualisation is needed for diagnosis.

**Mx**
- **Medical**: NSAIDs + paracetamol; COCP or progestogen-only pill if analgesics ineffective; GnRH analogues (induce pseudomenopause)
- **Surgical**: laparoscopic excision or ablation of endometriosis ± adhesions (↑chances of conception); ovarian cystectomy (for endometriomas)

**P**: mean of 10 years' delay from symptom onset to therapeutic intervention.

## Fibroids (uterine leiomyomata)

**D**: benign tumours of the uterus, primarily composed of smooth muscle and fibrous connective tissue.

**R**: ↑BMI, age ~40s, black ethnicity, ↓vitamin D.

**A/P**: ?monoclonal tumours, promoted by hormonal changes.

**S/smx**
- May be asymptomatic
- Menorrhagia ± iron deficiency anaemia
- Mass effect: lower abdominal cramping pain (often during menses), bloating, urinary frequency
- Subfertility
- Rarely, polycythaemia (autonomous erythropoietin production)

**Ix**: transvaginal US.

**Mx**
- If asymptomatic: watch and wait
- **Menorrhagia**: levonorgestrel IUS (useful if wanting contraception too, but not if uterine cavity is distorted); COCP; progesterone (PO/IM); NSAIDs (e.g. mefenamic acid); tranexamic acid
- **To shrink/remove fibroids**: GnRH agonists (short-term only — SE + bone mineral density loss); ulipristal acetate (not often used — liver toxicity risk); myomectomy (laparotomy/laparoscopy/hysteroscopy); hysteroscopic endometrial ablation; hysterectomy; uterine artery embolisation

**P**: fibroids generally regress after menopause. May rarely undergo red degeneration during pregnancy (haemorrhage).

## Chronic pelvic pain

Descriptive term for lower abdominal pain >6 months, not exclusively associated with menses, intercourse, or pregnancy.

**DDx**: endometriosis, PID, fibroids, adenomyosis, adhesions, pelvic congestion, Mittelschmerz. Non-gynae DDx: irritable bowel syndrome, interstitial cystitis.

**Ix**: guided by symptoms; laparoscopy may reveal cause but is normal in 30%.

**Mx**: if cyclical, consider COCP to suppress ovaries; GnRH analogues can induce temporary menopause to determine if symptoms are hormone-related.

### Mittelschmerz
**D**: mid-cycle menstrual pain around the time of ovulation. Usually affects teenagers and older women. **Mx**: analgesics (NSAIDs, paracetamol).

### Dyspareunia
**D**: pain during intercourse.

**DDx of superficial dyspareunia**: infection (ulcers, discharge), vaginal dryness (postmenopause, breastfeeding), lack of sexual stimulation, scarring (FGM, postpartum perineal repair), dermatographism.

**DDx of deep dyspareunia**: endometriosis, pelvic infections.

### Adenomyosis
**D**: endometrial glands and stroma present within the myometrium, causing myometrial hypertrophy.
- Can be asymptomatic or cause dysmenorrhoea, menorrhagia ± uterine enlargement/bogginess
- **Ix**: transvaginal US/MRI
- **Mx**: if symptomatic, tranexamic acid for menorrhagia; trial of GnRH agonists or uterine artery embolisation; definitive Mx: hysterectomy
