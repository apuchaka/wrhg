## Subfertility

**D**: no strict definition; major guidelines agree fertility is "delayed" if a woman of reproductive age has not conceived after 1 year of unprotected vaginal intercourse or therapeutic donor insemination.

**Causes**

| Cause | Prevalence |
|---|---|
| Anovulation (→17.01) | 21% — 2° premature ovarian failure, Turner syndrome, surgery, chemo, PCOS, endocrine issues, Kallmann syndrome, hyperprolactinaemia |
| Male factor (→17.05) | 25% |
| Tubal factor | 15–20% |
| Endometriosis | 6–8% |
| Unexplained | 28% |

**Ix**
- Detailed history from both partners
- STI screening
- Baseline hormonal profile, TSH, prolactin, testosterone
- Day 21 progesterone ("mid-cycle progesterone") — blood sample taken 7 days before expected period, to confirm ovulation
- Semen analysis (see below) — if abnormal, lifestyle changes + multivitamin (selenium, zinc, vitamin C), repeat in 3 months
- Transvaginal US — rule out masses, fibroids, polyps; helps confirm PCOS
- Hysterosalpingogram — contrast XR showing uterine anatomy and tubal patency (may cause period-like cramps/tubal spasm causing false +ve; can be done as US instead)
- Laparoscopy and dye test — gold standard for tubal patency assessment; 1st line if strong clinical suspicion of tubal abnormality

**Lifestyle modifications**: normalise weight, healthy diet, stop smoking/recreational drugs, drink within recommended limits, regular exercise, regular intercourse every 2–3 days. No proven benefit from ovulation monitors (↑stress).

### Medical Mx — ovarian induction
- Weight loss/gain as appropriate
- **Clomifene citrate** on day 2–6 of cycle — 10% multiple pregnancy rate; can cause hot flushes, labile mood (stop immediately if severe headache or visual disturbance); only use for 6–12 cycles (linked to ovarian cancer); requires follicular monitoring by US (hyperstimulation risk)
- **Laparoscopic ovarian drilling**: only for PCOS patients — small holes drilled into each ovary to ↓LH and restore feedback mechanisms; successful in 50%, lasts 12–18 months
- **Gonadotrophins**: used for clomifene-resistant PCOS, or low oestrogen with normal FSH
- **Metformin** (unlicensed): used in PCOS, less effective than weight loss

### Surgical Mx
- Tubal disease: tubal catheterisation or hysteroscopic cannulation (high ectopic pregnancy rate)
- Endometriosis: surgical Mx
- Intrauterine adhesions: hysteroscopic adhesiolysis

## In vitro fertilisation (IVF)

**D**: process where an oocyte is combined with sperm in vitro (outside the body).
- Oocyte production stimulated and oocytes collected
- Oocytes fertilised with harvested sperm — **intracytoplasmic sperm injection** (sperm directly inserted via micropipette) or older petri dish method
- Fertilised embryo reintroduced into uterus
- Embryos may be screened via pre-implantation genetic diagnosis (offered to those with previous termination for serious genetic disease, or personal/FHx of serious genetic disease — carries risk to the embryo)
- Luteal support via progestogens; pregnancy test 2w later

**Indications**: tubal disease, male factor subfertility, endometriosis, anovulation not responding to clomifene, subfertility 2° ↑maternal age, unexplained subfertility >2y.

> [!info] Localised for Australia — verified against Services Australia and current fertility provider information, Aug 2026. **Australia's funding model is structurally different from the UK's NHS system, not just numerically different**: rather than free provision gated by strict eligibility criteria (age caps, BMI limits, non-smoker status, no-existing-children rules), Australia uses a **Medicare rebate system** — patients pay upfront and receive a partial rebate, with an Extended Medicare Safety Net providing a larger capped rebate once an annual out-of-pocket threshold is reached; out-of-pocket "gap" costs typically remain even with the rebate.
> - **Core eligibility:** diagnosed as medically infertile by a fertility specialist, with a valid referral (GP referrals last 12 months; specialist referrals last 3 months) — there is **no BMI limit, no smoking-status requirement, and no "must have no existing children" rule** under Medicare, unlike the UK NHS criteria.
> - **No limit on the number of treatment cycles/rounds** under Medicare (genuinely different from the UK's typical 1–3 cycle NHS cap).
> - **Age:** generally no fixed Medicare age limit, though this varies — **Victoria specifically caps first-time IVF access at 46 years and embryo transfer at the patient's 51st birthday**, a state-specific rule worth knowing given it doesn't apply uniformly nationally; check current criteria for the relevant state.
> - Some states offer additional rebate schemes on top of Medicare (e.g. NSW's Affordable IVF Initiative).
> - Surrogacy arrangements are specifically **not** eligible for Medicare rebates.
> - Other options: adoption, fostering; surrogacy (altruistic surrogacy is legal in all Australian states/territories, but commercial surrogacy is not — genuinely complex, state-specific law; seek specific legal advice rather than treating this as a simple alternative).

### Other fertility options

| Option | Use |
|---|---|
| Donor insemination | Male partner azoospermia, failed surgical sperm retrieval, high genetic/HIV transmission risk, female with no male partner |
| Intrauterine insemination | Male factor subfertility, coital difficulties, unexplained subfertility, same-sex couples; can combine with ovarian stimulation |
| In vitro maturation | Immature eggs collected and matured in lab, then fertilised; used in PCOS (avoids OHSS risk) |
| Ooplasmic/nuclear transfer | Nucleus of one oocyte inserted into cytoplasm of another (with its mitochondrial DNA); used for female couples |
| Percutaneous epididymal sperm aspiration | Sperm removal via needle into epididymis |
| Pregnancy by ovary transplant | — |

### Semen analysis

> [!info] Normal semen parameters
> - Performed after 3–5 days abstinence; sample delivered to lab within 1h
> - Volume >1.5mL
> - pH >7.2
> - Concentration >15 million/mL
> - Vitality (live sperm) >58%
> - Normal forms >4%
> - Progressive motility >32%
> - Total motility >40%

## Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)

**D**: systemic disease complicating ovulation induction or superovulation.

**R**: young age, low BMI, polycystic ovaries, previous OHSS. Less seen with clomifene; more with gonadotrophin or hCG treatment.

**A/P**: vasoactive products (especially VEGF), oestrogens, and progesterone present in higher concentrations due to ovulation induction → ↑membrane permeability and loss of intravascular fluid → ↑blood concentration and hypercoagulability. Fluid accumulates in peritoneal and pleural spaces.

> [!info] RCOG severity classification
> | Grade | Features |
> |---|---|
> | Mild | Abdominal pain, abdominal bloating |
> | Moderate | + N&V, US evidence of ascites |
> | Severe | + clinical evidence of ascites, oliguria, Hct >45%, hypoproteinaemia |
> | Critical | + thromboembolism, ARDS, anuria, tense ascites |

**Mx**
- **Mild/moderate**: outpatient treatment — analgesia (avoid NSAIDs); avoid strenuous activity/intercourse (ovarian torsion risk); continue progesterone luteal support, avoid hCG; review by assisted conception unit every 2–3 days
- **Severe**: may need admission — daily bloods, strict fluid balance, daily assessment of ascites/weight/legs, thromboprophylaxis (stockings, LMWH), paracentesis for ascites ± IV albumin replacement
- **Critical**: admit ASAP + senior help + consider ICU
