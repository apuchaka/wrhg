## HIV in children

- In sub-Saharan countries, ~40% of all under-5 mortality is due to AIDS.
- Breastfeeding increases transmission risk by 50%. 2/3 of infections occur at delivery.

### Neonatal management

> [!info] Verified against the Australian HIV guidelines (hiv.guidelines.org.au, ASID/BHIVA-based with specific Australian annotations), Aug 2026 — **the core risk-stratified approach below is confirmed consistent with current Australian practice**: zidovudine monotherapy for low-risk infants (undetectable/well-suppressed maternal viral load) versus multi-drug prophylaxis for high-risk infants (detectable maternal viral load, insufficient maternal treatment, or known viral resistance) is genuinely the current Australian approach, not a UK-specific protocol needing wholesale replacement. Some genuinely current Australian-specific refinements worth noting: newer regimens increasingly favour tenofovir/integrase-inhibitor-based combinations over older zidovudine-heavy multi-drug regimens for better tolerability where multi-drug prophylaxis is needed; a shorter 2-week regimen may be appropriate for infants at very low transmission risk meeting specific criteria; and infant HIV testing timing/follow-up should be confirmed against the current Australian schedule directly (testing at day 1, and at intervals through the exposure period) rather than assumed identical to the UK timing this entry was originally written against, which it no longer carries. The exclusive formula feeding advice and PCP prophylaxis principle remain consistent with Australian guidance.

- Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for 4 weeks, starting <4h from birth: zidovudine only if maternal viral load <50 copies; 3-drug ART otherwise.
- Exclusive formula feeding from birth.
- Initiate PCP prophylaxis with co-trimoxazole from 4 weeks if tests are positive or equivocal.
- Delay BCG until confirmed HIV-negative, unless high risk of TB exposure.
- Other vaccinations per schedule.

### Neonatal testing

- HIV DNA PCR ± RNA PCR at birth, 6 weeks, 12 weeks. HIV antibodies at 18 months of age.
- Monthly testing if the infant is breastfeeding.
- Sensitivity of PCR testing increases to near 100% at 3 months of age, but interpret with caution.
- All clear if tests are negative at 18 months.

### Consider HIV in children with

> [!warning] Suspicion triggers: pyrexia of unknown origin; lymphadenopathy; hepatosplenomegaly; persistent diarrhoea; parotid enlargement; shingles; ↓platelets; clubbing; extensive molluscum; recurrent slow-to-clear infections; faltering growth; TB, pneumocystosis, toxoplasmosis, cryptococcosis, histoplasmosis, CMV, lymphocytic interstitial pneumonia (LIP). Also remember seroconversion illness.

- **Prognosis:** by age 3, ~50% mortality with early-onset opportunistic infections, compared to 3% with no HIV.
- Ensure a full course of vaccines + pneumococcus + annual influenza.
