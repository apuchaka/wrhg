## Cow's milk protein intolerance

- **D:** immediate or delayed reaction to proteins present in cow's milk. Occurs in 3–6% of all children.
- **S/Smx:** presents in the first 3 months of exposure to formula milk (containing cow's milk). Regurgitation, vomiting, diarrhoea. Urticaria, atopic eczema. "Colic" symptoms — irritability, crying. Wheezing, chronic cough. Rarely, angioedema and anaphylaxis.
- **Ix:** usually clinical diagnosis ± skin prick/patch testing, total IgE and specific IgE for cow's milk protein.
- **Mx:**
  - Extensively hydrolysed formula, or amino acid-based formula.
  - If breastfed, continue breastfeeding; eliminate cow's milk from the maternal diet + consider calcium supplements. Use extensively hydrolysed formula when stopping breastfeeding, until 12 months of age and for ≥6 months.
- **P:** usually self-resolving by 3–5 years old.

## GORD in children

- **R:** preterm babies, neuro disorders.
- **S/Smx:** presents in the first 8 weeks of life. Regurgitation, vomiting after feeds or when laid flat.
- **Ix:** usually clinical diagnosis.
- **Mx:**
  - 30° heads-up position while feeding.
  - Babies should still sleep on their backs (↓risk of cot death).
  - Ensure the baby is not overfed; consider a trial of smaller and more frequent feeds.
  - Trial of thickened formula.
  - Trial of alginate therapy (not at the same time as thickened formula).
  - PPI trial ONLY if: unexplained feeding difficulties; distressed behaviour; faltering growth.

## Infantile colic

- **D:** excessive crying and fussing in children. Occurs in 20% of infants.
- **S/Smx:** usually <3 months old. Bouts of excessive crying; pulling up legs; usually worse in the evening.
- **Mx:** watch and wait. **Simeticone and lactase are not recommended** — see the box below: the evidence against them is general rather than UK-specific, and Australian Prescriber and RCH Melbourne reach the same position. (Previously attributed to NICE in this line, which read as though the recommendation were a UK one.)

> [!info] Verified against Australian Prescriber's infantile colic review and RCH Melbourne's GORD in infants clinical practice guideline, Aug 2026 — **both flagged recommendations are confirmed genuinely evidence-based, not UK-specific stances**: simethicone and lactase are both confirmed ineffective for colic by the current Australian Prescriber review (simethicone specifically shown ineffective despite widespread use), and RCH Melbourne's own guideline confirms PPIs have no evidence for empiric/diagnostic use in irritable infants and are associated with real adverse effects (increased infection risk, fractures, micronutrient deficiencies) — supporting the same cautious PPI-threshold approach already in this entry. Alginate therapy (Gaviscon Infant) has some supporting evidence per RCH for reducing visible regurgitation specifically. Probiotics/prebiotics are explicitly not recommended per RCH despite some conflicting smaller trial evidence for specific strains (e.g. L. reuteri) in colic specifically — reasonable to mention as an emerging area rather than a recommended treatment. This is a case where the underlying evidence, not just the guideline body, genuinely supports the approach already described — no correction needed to the substance, though "NICE does not recommend" as a citation should be understood as "the evidence doesn't support," a distinction worth making since the evidence itself (not just NICE's interpretation of it) is what's genuinely internationally consistent.
