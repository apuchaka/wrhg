## Fragile X syndrome

- **D:** X-linked recessive trinucleotide repeat disorder. The most common monogenic cause of autism spectrum disorder.
- **R:** family history. **A/P:** CGG repetition within the fragile X mental retardation 1 gene → ↓gene expression.
- **S/Smx:** not apparent in newborns; symptoms develop in early childhood (average diagnosis at ~32 months, or 2 years 8 months). Characteristic facies — elongated, narrow face, broad forehead and philtrum, high-arched palate, protruding/large ears.
- **May present due to:** autistic behaviours (e.g. hand flapping, repetitive tasks); poor eye contact; developmental delay; seizures; psychiatric symptoms (anxiety, depression, ADHD); sleep disturbance; aggression.
- In males, macro-orchidism is a hallmark of FXS. A Prader-Willi-like phenotype may also be observed.
- **P:** depending on severity, some patients can lead relatively normal lives.
- **Premutation carriers** have less severe cognitive deficits BUT a high risk of developing Fragile X-associated tremor/ataxia syndrome (FXTAS) — presents in the 6th decade of life with tremors, ataxia, and neuropsychiatric conditions.

## Achondroplasia

- **D:** autosomal dominantly inherited skeletal dysplastic disorder resulting in disproportionately short stature.
- **R:** family history, ↑parental age (for de novo mutations).
- **A:** de novo mutation in 70–80% of cases. **P:** gain of function mutation in the FGFR3 gene → inhibits chondrocyte proliferation → ↓endochondral bone formation, growth restriction, bone shortening, etc.
- **S/Smx:** distinctive craniofacial features — macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface retrusion, saddle nose deformity. Disproportionate short stature with shortening of arms & legs. Shortening of fingers and toes (brachydactyly) + trident hands. Kyphoscoliosis. Accentuated lumbar lordosis.
- **P:** ↑mortality in childhood. Mean lifespan ~61 years old (~10 years less than the general population).

## Noonan syndrome

- **D:** autosomal dominant inherited disorder characterised by short stature and congenital heart disease. 1:1000–2500 births.
- **R:** family history, ↑paternal age. **A:** gain of function mutation in various genes.
- **S/Smx** (sometimes described as "male Turner's syndrome"): short stature; webbed neck; pectus carinatum or excavatum; widely spaced nipples. Facial features: ptosis, triangular-shaped face, low-set ears. Cardiac: pulmonary valve stenosis. Haematological: factor XI deficiency.
- **Ix:** ECG + echo.
- **Mx:** monitor for cardiac complications. Cryptorchidism — refer to urology. If poor growth, refer to endocrinology (may start on growth hormone).
- **P:** the majority have normal lives; prognosis depends on the type and severity of cardiac disease.

## Marfan syndrome

- **D:** autosomal dominant inherited disorder of connective tissue characterised by loss of elastic tissue. 1:3000 births.
- **R:** family history of Marfan's, family history of aortic dissection or aneurysm, ↑paternal age (in de novo mutations).
- **A:** inherited in 75%, de novo in 25%. **P:** mutation on the FBN1 gene on chromosome 15 → ↓fibrillin-1.
- **S/Smx:**
  - Physical: tall with long arms; high-arched palate; long fingers (arachnodactyly); pectus excavatum; pes planus; scoliosis >20°.
  - Cardiac: dilation of the aortic sinuses (90%) — aortic aneurysms, aortic dissection, aortic regurgitation; mitral valve prolapse (75%).
  - Lungs: repeated pneumothoraces.
  - Eyes: upward lens dislocation; blue sclera; myopia.
  - Others: dural ectasia (ballooning of the dural sac at the lumbosacral level).
- **Ix:** aortic root imaging (echo, thorax CT, or thorax MRI); visualising the descending aorta (abdominal US, CT, or MRI); CXR for pneumothorax.
- **Mx:**
  - Aortic monitoring.
  - In patients with aortic dilation, beta-blockers used to prevent further dilation — if contraindicated (e.g. asthma), losartan or verapamil may be used. If aortic diameter >4.5cm, or symptomatic (e.g. chest pain), consider surgery.
  - Orthopaedic correction for severe skeletal deformities.
