## 0.1 The Preventive Consultation

> [!tip] Most prevention happens opportunistically
> **Patients rarely book an appointment for prevention. They come about a sore throat, a repeat prescription or a certificate — and the preventive work is done in the margins of that consultation.**
> **The practical approach is a LIFECYCLE model: what should be considered at this person's age, sex and risk profile, checked opportunistically and recorded so it is not repeated unnecessarily.**
> **The RACGP "RED BOOK" is the Australian reference for what to do, at what age, and how often.** `UNVERIFIED — all specific recommendations.`
> **Medicare items exist to fund structured preventive work — health assessments for defined groups (an age-based assessment for older people, the ABORIGINAL AND TORRES STRAIT ISLANDER HEALTH ASSESSMENT, assessments for people with intellectual disability and for refugees), plus chronic disease management planning.** `UNVERIFIED — item numbers and eligibility.` Cross-refer [[AU1_Australian_Health_Context_and_ATSI_Health]] 0.1.

> [!danger] Know when to STOP screening
> **This is the least-taught part of prevention and it matters increasingly with age.**
> **Screening only benefits someone who lives long enough for the LEAD TIME to elapse — the years between detecting a disease early and it causing harm.**
> **SO SCREENING A PERSON WHOSE LIFE EXPECTANCY IS SHORTER THAN THAT LEAD TIME EXPOSES THEM TO ALL OF THE HARMS — the anxiety, the false positives, the biopsy, the treatment complications — WITH NO POSSIBILITY OF BENEFIT.**
> **The consequence: continuing routine cancer screening in a frail 88-year-old, or in someone with advanced heart failure or metastatic disease, is not thoroughness. It is harm.**
> **The conversation is difficult because stopping feels like giving up, and it is easier to keep ticking the box.** **A usable framing: "this test is designed to find something that would cause you trouble in ten or fifteen years — and I'd rather we spent our time on what's affecting you now."**
> **This is QUATERNARY PREVENTION — protecting patients from unnecessary medicalisation — and it belongs alongside the other three levels.** Cross-refer [[PH1_Population_Health_and_Research_Literacy]] 0.6 and [[GER1_Comprehensive_Geriatric_Assessment]] 0.2.

## 0.2 Cardiovascular and Metabolic Risk

> [!danger] Treat absolute risk, not individual numbers
> **THE CENTRAL PRINCIPLE OF CARDIOVASCULAR PREVENTION.**
> **A cholesterol of 6.5 means something entirely different in a 35-year-old non-smoking woman than in a 68-year-old man who smokes and has diabetes — and treating the number rather than the person's overall risk both over-treats the first and under-treats the second.**
> **Australia uses an ABSOLUTE CARDIOVASCULAR RISK calculation, estimating the probability of a cardiovascular event over a defined period, from a defined starting age — and that starting age is EARLIER for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, because cardiovascular disease occurs earlier.**
> **THE AUSTRALIAN CALCULATOR HAS RECENTLY BEEN REVISED, with changes to the inputs, the risk categories and the treatment thresholds.** `UNVERIFIED — use the current calculator and the current thresholds; do not rely on remembered figures.`
> **THE GROUPS WHO ARE AUTOMATICALLY HIGH RISK AND DO NOT NEED CALCULATING:**
> **· Established cardiovascular disease.**
> **· Diabetes with defined additional features (albuminuria, or reduced eGFR, or above a certain age or duration).**
> **· Moderate to severe CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE.**
> **· FAMILIAL HYPERCHOLESTEROLAEMIA.**
> **· Extreme single risk factors above defined thresholds.**
> **Calculating a risk score for someone already in one of these categories wastes time and can falsely reassure.** `UNVERIFIED — the qualifying criteria.`

> [!warning] Familial hypercholesterolaemia is common, under-diagnosed, and a cascade opportunity
> **An autosomal dominant condition causing lifelong elevated LDL cholesterol and premature cardiovascular disease — and it is substantially under-diagnosed.**
> **SUSPECT IT WITH: a very high LDL cholesterol · a personal or FAMILY history of PREMATURE cardiovascular disease (before about 55 in men, 60 in women) · TENDON XANTHOMATA (particularly Achilles) · corneal arcus in a young person · and xanthelasma at a young age.**
> **THE POINT THAT MAKES IT WORTH FINDING: CASCADE TESTING.** **Each index case has first-degree relatives with a one-in-two chance of carrying it — so diagnosing one person identifies a family, and treating them from a young age dramatically alters lifetime risk.**
> **Finding it in a 45-year-old is valuable. Finding it in their children is transformative.** `UNVERIFIED — diagnostic criteria and lipid thresholds.`

> [!tip] The rest of metabolic screening
> **· TYPE 2 DIABETES — risk-stratified screening, with AUSDRISK used to identify who to test.** **Screen earlier and more often in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, those from high-risk ancestries, with a family history, previous gestational diabetes, obesity, or on antipsychotics.** Cross-refer [[I2_Diabetes_and_Glucose_Disorders]] 0.1 and [[N3_Psychosis_and_Antipsychotics]] 0.7.
> **· CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE — eGFR AND urine ACR TOGETHER, because the two axes independently predict risk and eGFR alone misses albuminuric disease.** Screen those with diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, obesity, smoking, a family history, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. Cross-refer [[H3_Acute_Kidney_Injury_and_CKD]] 0.4.
> **· Blood pressure, weight and waist circumference, smoking, alcohol, physical activity and diet.**
> **· OSTEOPOROSIS and fracture risk** — cross-refer [[GER1_Comprehensive_Geriatric_Assessment]] 0.6, remembering that a minimal-trauma fracture is itself the diagnosis.
> `UNVERIFIED — all screening intervals and thresholds.`

## 0.3 Cancer Screening in Practice

> [!tip] The Australian programmes — and what actually limits them
> **· BOWEL — the NATIONAL BOWEL CANCER SCREENING PROGRAM mails immunochemical faecal occult blood tests to eligible ages.**
> **PARTICIPATION IS THE LIMITING FACTOR, and it is low. A GP's explicit endorsement measurably improves uptake — so mentioning the kit that is sitting unopened at home is a genuine intervention.**
> **AND: A POSITIVE iFOBT REQUIRES COLONOSCOPY. Delays in that pathway are a documented problem, and a positive result that is not followed up is worse than not screening.** **Check that it happened.** Cross-refer [[C5_Bowel_Habit__Obstruction_and_Distension]] 0.5.
> **· CERVICAL — now HPV-BASED with a longer interval, and with SELF-COLLECTION available.**
> **SELF-COLLECTION IS THE SIGNIFICANT DEVELOPMENT: it has improved participation among never-screened and under-screened women — including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander women, women from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, survivors of sexual assault, and gender-diverse people for whom a speculum examination is a genuine barrier.** **Offer it.** Cross-refer [[O6_Sexual_and_Reproductive_Health]] 0.2.
> **· BREAST — BreastScreen Australia, free mammography for asymptomatic women in a target age range.**
> **· LUNG — a national programme for high-risk smokers and former smokers has been introduced.**
> **· PROSTATE — NOT a population screening programme.** PSA testing is a shared decision, requiring a discussion of the overdiagnosis and overtreatment problem before the blood is taken. **And remember a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor halves the measured PSA.** Cross-refer [[H2_LUTS__Retention_and_Incontinence]] 0.2.
> **· SKIN — Australia has NO population screening programme despite the highest melanoma rates in the world.** Practice is opportunistic examination in high-risk individuals plus education in self-examination. Cross-refer [[G5_Lumps__Ulcers_and_Nails]] 0.1.
> `UNVERIFIED — all ages, intervals and eligibility.`

> [!danger] Screening is for asymptomatic people
> **A symptomatic patient needs DIAGNOSTIC assessment, not a screening test — and the two pathways are different.**
> **A woman with a breast lump goes for triple assessment, NOT to BreastScreen. A patient with rectal bleeding goes for colonoscopy, NOT for an iFOBT. A woman with postcoital bleeding needs the cervix examined and a colposcopy referral, NOT a routine screening test.**
> **AND A NORMAL RECENT SCREENING TEST DOES NOT EXCLUDE CANCER IN A SYMPTOMATIC PATIENT** — interval cancers occur, and screening tests are calibrated for populations, not for the person in front of you with a symptom.
> Cross-refer [[O7_Breast_Disease]] 0.5 and [[O4_Abnormal_and_Menstrual_Bleeding]] 0.3.

> [!warning] Who falls outside the population programmes
> **Population screening is designed for average-risk people. These groups need an individualised, usually more intensive pathway, and they are routinely missed because the mailed invitation is the default:**
> **· A strong FAMILY HISTORY or a known genetic syndrome — LYNCH SYNDROME, familial adenomatous polyposis, BRCA1/2, PALB2 — requiring earlier, more frequent and different-modality surveillance and referral to a familial cancer service.**
> **· A PREVIOUS CANCER, which changes both surveillance and second-primary risk.**
> **· IMMUNOSUPPRESSION, particularly ORGAN TRANSPLANT RECIPIENTS, who need intensive skin surveillance from the time of transplantation.** Cross-refer [[G5_Lumps__Ulcers_and_Nails]] 0.3.
> **· Previous CHEST RADIOTHERAPY, particularly in adolescence, requiring early and intensive breast surveillance.** Cross-refer [[O7_Breast_Disease]] 0.1.
> **· Specific exposures — asbestos, silica, aromatic amines.**
> **· People with severe mental illness and people with intellectual disability, who have markedly LOWER screening participation and worse cancer outcomes — a genuine equity gap that requires active facilitation rather than a mailed letter.** Cross-refer [[N3_Psychosis_and_Antipsychotics]] 0.7.

## 0.4 Immunisation

> [!tip] The framework
> **The NATIONAL IMMUNISATION PROGRAM provides funded vaccines by age and risk category; the AUSTRALIAN IMMUNISATION HANDBOOK is the authoritative reference; and the AUSTRALIAN IMMUNISATION REGISTER records vaccinations across the whole lifespan.**
> **CHECK THE REGISTER RATHER THAN RELYING ON RECALL.** **Patients and parents routinely believe they are up to date when they are not, and the register is the only reliable record — particularly for adults, catch-up schedules and people who have moved between states or countries.**
> **The broad structure: childhood schedule · adolescent (HPV, dTpa, meningococcal) · adult (influenza annually, COVID-19, pneumococcal, herpes zoster, dTpa) · PREGNANCY · Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander-specific and earlier schedules · MEDICAL RISK groups · occupational · and travel.**
> `UNVERIFIED — the entire schedule, ages, intervals and funding.`

> [!danger] The groups where getting it wrong causes real harm
> **· PREGNANCY — dTpa and INFLUENZA are recommended in every pregnancy.** **Maternal pertussis vaccination protects the NEWBORN in the window before their own immunisation, and it is the reason infants stop dying of pertussis.** **COCOONING — vaccinating parents, grandparents and carers — extends that protection.** Cross-refer [[M2_Fever_in_Children]] 0.6 and [[O2_Later_Pregnancy_and_Fetal]].
> **· ASPLENIA AND HYPOSPLENISM — functional or surgical, including sickle cell disease and coeliac disease.** **These patients need specific vaccination (pneumococcal, meningococcal, Hib, influenza), often ANTIBIOTIC PROPHYLAXIS, an alert card or bracelet, and education about seeking urgent care with fever — because overwhelming post-splenectomy infection kills within hours.** **This is frequently not arranged after an emergency splenectomy, when nobody owns the follow-up.** Cross-refer [[K3_Exposure__Tuberculosis__HIV_and_Immunodeficiency]] 0.5 and [[J2_Haemoglobinopathy_and_Haemolysis]] 0.5.
> **· IMMUNOSUPPRESSION — LIVE VACCINES (MMR, varicella, zoster live formulations, yellow fever, BCG, oral typhoid) ARE CONTRAINDICATED in significant immunosuppression and in pregnancy.** **Ideally vaccinate BEFORE starting immunosuppressive therapy, which requires planning at the point the treatment is being considered — and is routinely missed.**
> **· CHRONIC DISEASE — cardiac, respiratory, renal, hepatic and diabetes all attract additional recommendations.**
> **· OCCUPATIONAL — healthcare workers, including you: hepatitis B with documented serological response, MMR, varicella, dTpa and annual influenza.** **Know your own status.**

> [!warning] Two practical points
> **· EGG ALLERGY IS NO LONGER A CONTRAINDICATION TO INFLUENZA VACCINATION**, including in anaphylactic egg allergy, in current guidance — a change from older practice that still causes unnecessary exclusion. `UNVERIFIED — confirm current recommendations.`
> **· CATCH-UP is possible at any age, and an overdue schedule does not need restarting from the beginning.** **Use the Handbook's catch-up calculator rather than guessing, and take the opportunity when a person presents for anything else.**
> **Vaccine hesitancy communication — including why bluntly refuting myths can backfire, and why a presumptive approach improves uptake — is in [[PH1_Population_Health_and_Research_Literacy]] 0.6.**

## 0.5 Lifestyle Risk and Behaviour Change

> [!tip] The 5 A's, and why the last two matter most
> **· ASK — about the behaviour, routinely and without judgement.**
> **· ASSESS — the level of risk, and their readiness and confidence to change.**
> **· ADVISE — clearly, personally and briefly.**
> **· ASSIST — with a plan, pharmacotherapy, and referral.**
> **· ARRANGE — follow-up.**
> **Most clinicians do the first three and stop. ASSIST AND ARRANGE are where the effect is** — advice without support and without follow-up changes very little.

> [!danger] Smoking cessation is the highest-yield thing you will do
> **No other single intervention available to a generalist produces as much benefit across as many diseases.**
> **BRIEF ADVICE WORKS — even very short, structured advice measurably increases quit rates — and it is systematically under-delivered because clinicians assume the patient has heard it before, or fear damaging the relationship.**
> **WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS: PHARMACOTHERAPY PLUS BEHAVIOURAL SUPPORT, together.** **Combination nicotine replacement (a patch plus a fast-acting form), varenicline, or bupropion — combined with Quitline or equivalent behavioural support.** `UNVERIFIED — agents, availability and PBS criteria.`
> **FRAME RELAPSE CORRECTLY. Most people who successfully quit have made MULTIPLE previous attempts, and each attempt is progress rather than failure.** **Saying this explicitly — "most people take a few goes, and the ones before weren't wasted" — removes the shame that stops people trying again.**
> **ASK ABOUT VAPING SEPARATELY,** because people do not classify it as smoking, and the Australian regulatory position has changed substantially and continues to. Cross-refer [[M7_Adolescent_and_Behavioural_Health]] 0.4.
> **And identify the TEACHABLE MOMENTS — a new diagnosis, a pregnancy, an admission, an upcoming operation.** **The perioperative period is one of the most effective windows for cessation there is.** Cross-refer [[AN1_Perioperative_Care]] 0.1.

> [!warning] Motivational interviewing — the technique in brief
> **The evidence-based approach to behaviour change, and it works better than advice-giving.**
> **THE PRINCIPLES: express EMPATHY · develop DISCREPANCY between the person's behaviour and their own stated goals · ROLL WITH RESISTANCE rather than arguing against it · and support SELF-EFFICACY.**
> **THE PRACTICAL MOVES:**
> **· ASK PERMISSION — "would it be alright if we talked about your drinking for a minute?" This single step markedly reduces defensiveness.**
> **· ELICIT–PROVIDE–ELICIT — find out what they already know, offer information, then ask what they make of it. Far more effective than a lecture.**
> **· Use SCALING questions — "on a scale of one to ten, how important is it to you to stop? And why not a lower number?" — which elicits the person's own arguments for change rather than yours.**
> **· LET THE PATIENT VOICE THE REASONS. Change talk from the patient predicts change; change talk from the doctor does not.**
> **WHAT DOES NOT WORK, AND ACTIVELY HARMS: lecturing · moralising · scare tactics and graphic warnings delivered confrontationally · expressing disappointment · and arguing.** **These produce defensiveness, damage the therapeutic relationship, and make the person less likely to raise the topic again.**

> [!tip] The other lifestyle domains
> **· ALCOHOL — screen with AUDIT-C, use the NHMRC guidance on low-risk drinking, and deliver brief intervention, which has good evidence in non-dependent risky drinkers.** Cross-refer [[N2_Acute_Behavioural_Disturbance_and_Substance_Use]] 0.2.
> **· PHYSICAL ACTIVITY — prescribe it SPECIFICALLY (type, frequency, duration, intensity) rather than saying "try to be more active", and emphasise that SOME IS BETTER THAN NONE — the largest health gain is between doing nothing and doing a little.**
> **· NUTRITION AND WEIGHT — use non-stigmatising language, focus on BEHAVIOURS and health markers rather than on the number, and recognise that weight stigma in healthcare drives avoidance of care and worsens outcomes.** **Screen for binge eating disorder before recommending dietary restriction.** Cross-refer [[I5_Weight__Lipids_and_Fluid_Balance]] 0.1 and [[N8_Eating_Disorders]] 0.5.
> **· SUN PROTECTION — cross-refer [[G4_Chronic_Rash__Pruritus_and_Pigment]] 0.6. · SEXUAL HEALTH — cross-refer [[O6_Sexual_and_Reproductive_Health]]. · ORAL HEALTH, including asking about access — cross-refer [[F3_Throat__Voice_and_Oral]] 0.6. · SLEEP — cross-refer [[N7_Sleep_Disorders]]. · SOCIAL CONNECTION and loneliness, which are independent health risks. · FALLS PREVENTION — cross-refer [[GER1_Comprehensive_Geriatric_Assessment]] 0.5.**

## 0.6 Occupational Health, Certification and Driving

> [!warning] Take the occupational history — it is systematically omitted
> **"What do you do for work? What have you done in the past, including casual and student jobs? What are you exposed to? Does anyone else at work have this? ARE YOU BETTER ON DAYS OFF OR ON HOLIDAY?"**
> **That last question is the single most useful one, and it detects occupational asthma, dermatitis and many other work-related conditions.**
> **The conditions to think about: occupational LUNG disease including silicosis and asbestos-related disease (cross-refer [[RESP-X_Occupational_and_Chronic_Lung_Disease]] 0.2–0.3) · NOISE-INDUCED HEARING LOSS (cross-refer [[F1_Ear]] 0.4) · contact dermatitis (cross-refer [[G4_Chronic_Rash__Pruritus_and_Pigment]] 0.1) · musculoskeletal disorders · psychological injury and workplace bullying · needlestick and blood-borne virus exposure · and infectious risks.**
> **AND REMEMBER THE PUBLIC HEALTH DIMENSION: an index case implies a workplace hazard affecting OTHER WORKERS.** **Identifying it protects them, and it is the part clinicians most often omit.** Notification obligations to the work health and safety regulator may apply. `UNVERIFIED — SA requirements.`

> [!danger] Certify what they CAN do — prolonged absence from work is harmful
> **The evidence is clear and counterintuitive to many patients: WORK IS GENERALLY GOOD FOR PHYSICAL AND MENTAL HEALTH, and PROLONGED ABSENCE FROM WORK IS HARMFUL — associated with worse physical and mental health, social isolation, financial hardship, and a rapidly falling probability of ever returning.**
> **The likelihood of return to work falls steeply with time away, and after a period measured in months it becomes very low.**
> **THEREFORE: CERTIFICATES SHOULD SPECIFY SUITABLE DUTIES — what the person CAN do — rather than only certifying total incapacity.** **Modified hours, restricted tasks, avoiding specific activities, a graded return.**
> **A certificate for total unfitness, renewed repeatedly without a return-to-work plan, is not a neutral act — it contributes to the outcome.**
> **In South Australia, work-related injury is managed through ReturnToWorkSA processes, with specific certificate requirements.** `UNVERIFIED — current forms and requirements.` Cross-refer [[L6_Soft_Tissue_Injury_and_Mobility]] 0.5 and [[L4_Back_and_Neck_Pain]] 0.1.

> [!warning] Writing certificates honestly
> **· Base it on YOUR OWN ASSESSMENT of the patient.**
> **· State facts and your professional opinion, and distinguish between what the patient REPORTED and what you FOUND.**
> **· Be cautious about certifying retrospectively for a period during which you did not assess the patient — and if you do, make clear that it is based on history.**
> **· Do not be pressured into certifying something you do not believe. Patients sometimes ask, employers sometimes push, and the certificate carries your name.**
> **· A medical certificate is a legal document, and dishonest certification is a professional conduct matter.**
> **· Know when you are the TREATING DOCTOR versus an INDEPENDENT ASSESSOR — the roles are different and the conflict of interest is real.** **A treating doctor advocates for their patient within the bounds of honesty; an independent medical examiner does not have a therapeutic relationship and must say so to the person being assessed.**

> [!danger] Fitness to drive — assess and advise; the patient notifies
> **The Australian standard is Austroads' "ASSESSING FITNESS TO DRIVE", which sets out the medical standards for private and commercial licences.** `UNVERIFIED — all standards and non-driving periods.`
> **THE ROLE DIVISION MATTERS:**
> **· THE DOCTOR'S role is to ASSESS fitness against the standards, ADVISE the patient clearly, and DOCUMENT that advice.**
> **· THE DRIVER holds the legal obligation to NOTIFY the licensing authority of a condition affecting their driving.**
> **· AND SOUTH AUSTRALIA HAS SPECIFIC PROVISIONS regarding reporting by health professionals, which differ from other states.** `UNVERIFIED — check the SA provisions before acting.`
> **THE CONDITIONS THAT COME UP: SEIZURES and epilepsy (with defined non-driving periods, including after a first seizure — cross-refer [[D6_Seizures_and_Movement_Disorders]] 0.3) · SYNCOPE (cross-refer [[B4_Syncope__Collapse_and_Hypotension]]) · cardiac conditions and devices · DIABETES with hypoglycaemia unawareness or severe hypoglycaemia (cross-refer [[I2_Diabetes_and_Glucose_Disorders]] 0.4) · VISUAL standards, including acuity and fields (cross-refer [[E2_Vision_Loss_and_Disturbance]] 0.4) · DEMENTIA and cognitive impairment (cross-refer [[D2_Altered_Consciousness_and_Cognition]] 0.3) · obstructive SLEEP APNOEA with sleepiness (cross-refer [[RESP-X_Occupational_and_Chronic_Lung_Disease]] 0.5) · substance use · and SEDATING MEDICATIONS.**
> **COMMERCIAL STANDARDS ARE STRICTER, and the consequences for the patient are far greater — a person may lose their livelihood. That makes the conversation harder and makes documenting it more important, not less.**
> **DOCUMENT: what you assessed, what you advised, that you told them of their obligation to notify, and their response.** **This protects the patient, the public and you.** Cross-refer [[A10_Ethics__Capacity__Consent_and_Certification]] 0.6.

> [!tip] The health of the health worker — including yours
> **· IMMUNISATION and documented serology.**
> **· NEEDLESTICK AND BODY FLUID EXPOSURE — know the local protocol BEFORE you need it: wash, report immediately, source testing, risk assessment, and post-exposure prophylaxis where indicated, which is TIME-CRITICAL for HIV.** Cross-refer [[K3_Exposure__Tuberculosis__HIV_and_Immunodeficiency]] 0.5.
> **· FATIGUE AND SHIFT WORK — including the crash risk driving home after nights.** Cross-refer [[N7_Sleep_Disorders]] 0.3.
> **· HAVE YOUR OWN GENERAL PRACTITIONER, AND USE THEM.** **Doctors self-diagnose, self-prescribe, seek corridor consultations, and delay presenting — and they have worse health outcomes as a result, including for mental illness.** **Self-treatment and treating your own family is discouraged for good reasons: you cannot take an objective history, you skip the examination, and you avoid the difficult questions.**
> **· And know that AHPRA mandatory notification provisions have been amended specifically to reduce the barrier to doctors seeking help for their own health, particularly mental health — treating practitioners are not required to notify simply because a doctor is receiving treatment.** `UNVERIFIED — the current provisions.` Cross-refer [[A10_Ethics__Capacity__Consent_and_Certification]] 0.4.

**Ix:** **CHECK THE AUSTRALIAN IMMUNISATION REGISTER rather than asking** (*why:* recall is unreliable and adults are frequently overdue; *what:* the actual immunisation status). **ABSOLUTE CARDIOVASCULAR RISK using the current calculator — and identify the automatically high-risk groups first** (*why:* treating numbers rather than risk both over- and under-treats; *what:* the risk category and the treatment threshold). **eGFR AND urine ACR together in at-risk groups** (*why:* the two axes independently predict outcome and eGFR alone misses albuminuric disease; *what:* CKD stage). **AUSDRISK, then glucose or HbA1c** (*why:* targets testing to those who benefit; *what:* diabetes or prediabetes). **Lipids with consideration of FAMILIAL HYPERCHOLESTEROLAEMIA and cascade testing of relatives** (*why:* diagnosing one person identifies a family in whom early treatment transforms lifetime risk; *what:* very high LDL, tendon xanthomata, premature family history). **Confirm that a POSITIVE iFOBT has actually led to a COLONOSCOPY** (*why:* the follow-up gap is a documented failure and a positive test not acted upon is worse than no test; *what:* completed colonoscopy). **OCCUPATIONAL HISTORY with the "better on days off" question** (*why:* it detects work-related disease that is otherwise attributed to something else, and it protects other workers; *what:* the exposure). **Assessment against the AUSTROADS standards, with the advice documented** (*why:* the doctor assesses and advises while the driver notifies, and documentation protects everyone; *what:* fitness, and the conversation).
